## 1. U.S. Decentralized Statistical System and Agency Technical Delivery Channels

### Takeaway
Unlike countries with a single national statistical office, U.S. macroeconomic production is decentralized across 13 Principal Statistical Agencies coordinated by OMB—primarily BEA (GDP/PCE), BLS (CPI/employment), and the Census Bureau (retail/housing/durable goods)—each maintaining distinct REST APIs, bulk flat-file/table downloads, and post-2020 simultaneous web-release pipelines without physical media lock-ups.

### Cited Findings
- **Decentralized institutional structure:** The United States operates a decentralized federal statistical system rather than a single centralized national statistical office, overseen and coordinated by the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA), and the Chief Statistician of the United States — [Congressional Research Service, *The Federal Statistical System: Overview and Issues* (IN12197)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12197.html); [OMB, *Fundamental Responsibilities of Recognized Statistical Agencies and Units* (88 FR 56708, Aug. 18, 2023)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/08/18/2023-17664/fundamental-responsibilities-of-recognized-statistical-agencies-and-units).
- **Division of macroeconomic statistics across core agencies:** Within the 13 Principal Statistical Agencies (PSAs), core monetary-policy indicators are split across separate cabinet departments: the **Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA)** (Department of Commerce) compiles national income and product accounts including GDP, Personal Income and Outlays (PCE), Corporate Profits, and international accounts; the **Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)** (Department of Labor) compiles prices (CPI, PPI), labor markets (Employment Situation: CES payrolls and CPS household survey), Employment Cost Index (ECI), and JOLTS; and the **U.S. Census Bureau** (Department of Commerce) produces monthly/quarterly economic indicators including retail trade, new residential construction (housing starts/permits), durable goods/manufacturers' shipments (M3), wholesale trade, and merchandise trade — [CRS IN12197](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12197.html); [BEA Data Dissemination Practices](https://www.bea.gov/about/policies-and-information/data-dissemination); [U.S. Census Bureau, *Economic Indicators Time Series API*](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/economic-indicators.html).
- **BEA Data API (current as of April 2026):** BEA provides a single-endpoint HTTPS GET REST API at `https://apps.bea.gov/api/data` requiring a free 36-character `UserID` registered at `https://apps.bea.gov/api/signup/` (disposable email addresses are prohibited and subject to removal without warning). It exposes four metadata-discovery methods (e.g., `GETDATASETLIST`) and one data-retrieval method (`GetData`) across datasets including `NIPA` (standard NIPA tables covering GDP and PCE) and `NIUnderlyingDetail`, returning **JSON** (default) or **XML** (`ResultFormat`) with CORS and legacy JSONP support — [BEA, *BEA API for Data Retrieval User Guide* (April 20, 2026)](https://apps.bea.gov/api/_pdf/bea_web_service_api_user_guide.pdf).
- **BEA API rate limits and non-API channels:** BEA enforces three per-minute account rate limits: **100 requests/minute**, **100 MB of data volume/minute**, and **30 errors/minute**. Alongside the API, BEA distributes interactive web tables supporting CSV, Excel spreadsheet, and PDF downloads, and BEA's Dissemination Procedures explicitly bring API dissemination under the same release-time security and anti-DDoS controls as news releases — [BEA, *BEA API for Data Retrieval User Guide* (April 20, 2026)](https://apps.bea.gov/api/_pdf/bea_web_service_api_user_guide.pdf); [BEA, *For Developers*](https://www.bea.gov/developers); [BEA, *Updated Security and Release Procedures* (Dec. 2025)](https://www.bea.gov/system/files/2025-12/Security-and-Release-Procedures.pdf).
- **BLS Public Data API v1 and v2:** BLS operates two REST tiers: **v1** (`https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v1`, unregistered: **25 queries/day**, **25 series/query**, **10 years/query**) and **v2** (`https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2`, free email registration key: **500 queries/day**, **50 series/query**, **20 years/query**, optional net/percent change calculations, annual averages, and series catalog descriptions). Both versions enforce a burst limit of **50 requests per 10 seconds** and return JSON (with `.xlsx` Excel export options available in BLS data tools) — [BLS Public Data API Features](https://www.bls.gov/bls/api_features.htm); [BLS Developers API FAQs](https://www.bls.gov/developers/api_faqs.htm).
- **BLS bulk flat files and anti-bot enforcement:** For full-dataset/backfill loads exceeding API daily quotas, BLS hosts tab-delimited bulk flat files organized by two-letter survey prefix (e.g., `cu` for CPI-U, `ce` for CES payrolls, `ln` for CPS) at `https://download.bls.gov/pub/time.series/` (e.g., `ln.data.1.AllData`). Concurrently, BLS strictly enforces automated-retrieval usage rules on `www.bls.gov`, blocking non-conforming automated HTTP clients with HTTP 403 ("Automated retrieval programs (commonly called 'robots' or 'bots') can cause delays and interfere with other customers' timely access to information") — [BLS Developers API FAQs](https://www.bls.gov/developers/api_faqs.htm); [EconIndx / BLS Flat File Documentation via BLS time.series directory](https://www.bls.gov/bls/api_features.htm).
- **Census Bureau Economic Indicators API and bulk files:** Census provides the **Economic Indicators Time Series (EITS)** REST API at `https://api.census.gov/data/timeseries/eits/` (requiring a free Census API key) covering housing (`resconst`, `hv`), durable goods (`advm3`), retail/wholesale trade, construction, and international trade, supplemented by downloadable bulk datasets and data dictionaries at `www.census.gov/econ_datasets/` — [U.S. Census Bureau, *Economic Indicators (Time Series)*](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/economic-indicators.html).
- **Permanent end of physical press lock-up feeds (2020):** Historically, credentialed news organizations accessed PFEI releases 30 minutes early in the Department of Labor (DOL) physical lock-up room—used by BLS, ETA, BEA, and Census—allowing media vendors to preload computerized high-speed trading feeds. Following a DOL Office of Inspector General report flagging unfair commercial advantages for algorithmic trading clients, DOL first announced a ban on electronic devices inside the lock-up effective March 1, 2020 (`85 FR 7342`), suspended the room on March 20, 2020 due to COVID-19, and **permanently discontinued the DOL media lock-up facility effective June 3, 2020**, moving PFEI dissemination fully to simultaneous public release via agency websites, APIs, email lists, and social channels — [DOL/BLS Notice (Feb. 7, 2020, 85 FR 7342 / FR Doc. 2020-02463)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-02-07/pdf/2020-02463.pdf); [DOL/BLS Notice, *Announcing Discontinuation of the DOL Lock-Up Facility* (May 27, 2020, 85 FR 31810)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/05/27/2020-11297/announcing-discontinuation-of-the-dol-lock-up-facility-for-participating-news-media-organizations).

### Inferences
- Because U.S. macro indicators are split across three separate cabinet-level agencies with incompatible series-naming schemes, differing API quotas (BEA's 100 req/min vs. BLS's 500 req/day cap on v2), and divergent bulk-file conventions, institutional consumers like the Federal Reserve face substantial schema-harmonization and metadata-maintenance overhead—creating strong structural demand for both centralized internal time-series stores (such as FAME) and commercial/public aggregators (such as Haver Analytics and FRED).
- The 2020 abolition of physical press lock-ups removed a key institutional latency advantage previously enjoyed by commercial news wires over direct web/API consumers, shifting release-day ingestion competition to automated polling and high-availability web/API distribution at 8:30 a.m. ET.

### Gaps
- BLS's `www.bls.gov/developers/api_faqs.htm` and `download.bls.gov` endpoints return HTTP 403 to standard non-browser HTTP clients without whitelisted/conforming User-Agent headers, preventing direct live inspection of any 2025–2026 BLS API schema revisions beyond published v1/v2 specifications.
- Public BEA documentation does not enumerate whether BEA maintains dedicated non-public bulk FTP/SFTP or higher-quota API keys specifically for federal interagency consumers like the Federal Reserve.

## 2. Federal Reserve Board and FOMC Staff Ingestion (Haver Analytics, FAME, DDP/SDMX, and Ad Hoc Workflows)

### Takeaway
Publicly documented Federal Reserve Board infrastructure centers on internal **FIS FAME** time-series databases (holding tens of thousands of series mapped to a custom hierarchical nomenclature and SQL-backed operational metadata system), alongside **Haver Analytics** commercial feeds across the Federal Reserve System and a phased **2025–2027 retirement** of the Board's pioneering SDMX-ML **Data Download Program (DDP)** into St. Louis Fed's **FRED**.

### Cited Findings
- **Internal FAME production databases at the Federal Reserve Board:** Federal Reserve Board staff San Cannon and Meredith Krug documented that the Board's research divisions maintain two main production time-series databases in **FAME** containing approximately **62,000 time series** (52,000 domestic and 10,000 international; ~45,000 unique economic measures across frequencies) drawn from **more than 60 sources** and roughly **100 statistical releases** (including BLS PPI/CPI/CES/CPS, BEA NIPA/GDP, and Census new residential construction) — [San Cannon & Meredith Krug (Federal Reserve Board), *"Everything but the Kitchen Sink: Building a Metadata Repository for Time Series Data at the Federal Reserve Board,"* IASSIST Quarterly, Spring 2006](https://iassistquarterly.com/public/pdfs/iqvol301cannon.pdf).
- **Custom nomenclature, structural attributes, and operational ingestion metadata:** To standardize heterogeneous U.S. agency feeds inside FAME, Board staff maintain a hierarchical naming convention originally adapted in the 1980s from Townsend Greenspan (62 high-level roots expanding to 22,000 unique roots, up to four underscore modifiers, and a dot-frequency suffix—e.g., `PJP_G054_N.M` for monthly NSA BLS PPI for Electric Power), augmented by **15 custom FAME attributes** (`AGENCY`, `PUBLICATION`, `TABLE`, `LINE_NUMBER`, `UNITS`, `UPDATERS`, and second-level `TIME_STAMP`). Ingestion workflows are coordinated via an SQL-backed **Data Release Calendar** and a restricted-wiki **Data Documentation System** documenting source retrieval methods, updating programs, post-update scripts, revision procedures, and fallback methods when standard update channels fail, plus a **Data Sourcebook** for methodological reference metadata and a contemporaneous "real-time/snapshot" vintage database — [Cannon & Krug, IASSIST Quarterly (Spring 2006)](https://iassistquarterly.com/public/pdfs/iqvol301cannon.pdf).
- **Programmatic R and Python interfaces for FAME and FRB/US:** Board economist Jeff Hallman (`jeffrey.j.hallman@frb.gov`) authored and maintained the open-source **`fame` R package** on CRAN to read and write FAME time-series databases directly from R. For macroeconomic forecasting and FOMC policy simulations, the Board's flagship **FRB/US** general-equilibrium model (in use since 1996 and incorporating all major product- and income-side NIPA accounts) historically ran in commercial **EViews** and added a **Python** implementation in April 2022 using Board-developed model solution software — [CRAN `fame` Package Documentation (Jeff Hallman, FRB)](https://rdrr.io/cran/fame/); [Federal Reserve Board, *The FRB/US Project*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-about.htm).
- **Use of Haver Analytics across the Federal Reserve System and U.S. regulatory agencies:** Haver Analytics ingests raw releases from BEA, BLS, Census, and the Federal Reserve Board into standardized databases (such as `USECON` and `FFUNDS`) integrated with Excel, MATLAB, EViews, and institutional time-series platforms. Public Federal Reserve Bank outputs and U.S. financial-stability reports routinely cite **Haver Analytics** alongside primary BEA, BLS, and Board sources—including the Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland's daily **Inflation Nowcasting** (CPI and PCE) and the Treasury Office of Financial Research's 2025 Annual Report—and federal procurement records on SAM.gov document recurring government subscriptions to Haver Analytics economic database services — [Haver Analytics, *Our Data*](https://www.haver.com/our-data); [Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland, *Inflation Nowcasting*](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/inflation-nowcasting); [Office of Financial Research, *Calm Markets and Underlying Risks* (March 26, 2026)](https://www.financialresearch.gov/the-ofr-blog/2026/03/26/calm-markets-and-underlying-risks/); [SAM.gov Federal Contract Opportunity for Haver Analytics](https://sam.gov/opp/fd90de6ad08e4b8e8357d99926724931/view).
- **Data Download Program (DDP) — SDMX-ML pioneer (2006) now retiring into FRED (2025–2027):** On April 19, 2006, the Federal Reserve Board launched its **Data Download Program (DDP)**—the first central bank web application to deliver custom statistical packages in **SDMX-ML** (alongside CSV, Excel, and XML), starting with G.17, Z.1, Commercial Paper, and H.15. In **2025–2026**, the Board formally partnered with the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis to **retire DDP and consolidate public delivery into FRED**:
  - Interactive "View chart" feature retired **December 18, 2025**;
  - Custom "Build Your Package" option retired **November 2026** (announced July 16, 2026; confirmed retired November 12, 2026);
  - Preformatted data packages scheduled for retirement **March 31, 2027**;
  - Complete DDP page removal scheduled for **July 1, 2027**.
  The Board published a DDP-to-FRED Series Crosswalk (CSV, updated July 6, 2026), retains raw XML files on individual release pages, and directs API/bulk users to FRED's JSON/XML API and CSV/Excel downloads — [Federal Reserve Board Press Release (April 19, 2006)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20060419a.htm); [Federal Reserve Board, *Data Download Program and FRED Partnership* (updated Nov. 12, 2026)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/data-download-fred-information.htm).

### Inferences
- The Board's outbound architecture (retiring DDP into FRED while keeping raw XML on release pages) and inbound architecture (FAME + custom metadata/operational pipelines + Haver Analytics) reflect a hybrid model: specialized internal time-series databases and vendor feeds power confidential FOMC/Tealbook production, while public dissemination of Fed-produced data has been consolidated onto the St. Louis Fed's modern API-first FRED platform.
- Because the Board's FAME architecture documented in 2006 relied heavily on manual/scripted per-release operational procedures and custom nomenclature across ~100 releases, modernizing those pipelines alongside open-source Python/R modeling (such as FRB/US in Python) requires maintaining parallel wrappers or gradual migration rather than an abrupt cutover.

### Gaps
- **Non-public internal plumbing for Tealbook / FOMC staff:** The Board of Governors does not publicly disclose its current (2025–2026) internal ingestion split between Haver Analytics (`USECON`/DLX), Bloomberg/LSEG terminals, and direct BEA/BLS API or flat-file scripts for live FOMC Tealbook production, nor whether FAME remains the primary internal production database at the Board today or has been partially replaced by cloud/SQL warehouses (the Cannon & Krug primary architectural account is from 2006).
- **Procurement detail:** Publicly accessible SAM.gov listings do not expose the full line-item contract values or technical delivery SLAs of Board of Governors subscriptions to Haver Analytics, FIS FAME, Bloomberg, or LSEG (note that the Federal Reserve Board operates outside standard Federal Acquisition Regulation [FAR] executive-agency reporting in many procurement categories).
- **Ad hoc economist Excel/CSV workflows:** While Haver's DLX Excel add-in, FRED's Excel/CSV downloads, and FAME's Excel populator are standard tools across central banks, no public Federal Reserve Board audit quantifies how frequently Board economists pull ad hoc Excel/CSV extracts from BEA/BLS websites versus querying internal FAME/Haver databases.

## 3. Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis: FRED and ALFRED Ingestion Pipelines

### Takeaway
Maintained by the St. Louis Fed's Research Information Services / FRED Data Desk, **FRED** acts as a unified public aggregator ingesting hundreds of thousands of time series from over 100 sources (including BEA, BLS, Census, and now the Board's retiring DDP series) via a combination of automated pipelines (APIs, bulk files/FTP, structured transfers, and custom parsers) and human data-desk curation, with **ALFRED** preserving bi-temporal revision vintages.

### Cited Findings
- **Institutional ownership and aggregator role:** FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data) is operated by the Research Division's Research Information Services / **FRED Data Desk** at the **Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis** (historically led by figures such as Katrina Stierholz, Vice President and Director of Library and Research Information Services, and Keith Taylor, Director/Group VP overseeing Research Information Services and the Data Desk). Rather than conducting primary economic surveys, FRED aggregates hundreds of thousands of time series from more than 100 national, international, public, and private sources—including BEA (GDP, PCE), BLS (CPI, PPI, Employment Situation), Census (housing, retail trade), and the Federal Reserve Board — [Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, *Research Information Services*](https://www.stlouisfed.org/research/information-services); [St. Louis Fed, *2018 Annual Report: Go Figure with FRED* (April 16, 2019)](https://www.stlouisfed.org/news-releases/2019/04/16/st-louis-fed-2018-annual-report-go-figure-with-fred); [St. Louis Fed, *Have You Met FRED?*](https://www.stlouisfed.org/timely-topics/have-you-met-fred); [Washington Post, *"Meet FRED, every wonk's secret weapon"* (Aug. 1, 2014)](https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/storyline/wp/2014/08/01/meet-fred-every-wonks-secret-weapon/).
- **Hybrid automated ingestion plus human curation:** FRED's Data Desk tracks official source release calendars and uses automated data-ingestion pipelines (combining agency APIs, bulk file/FTP transfers, and custom parsers/scrapers) to pull and standardize newly released numbers from agencies such as BEA and BLS into FRED's relational schema organized by `sources`, `releases`, `release dates`, `categories`, and `series` — [St. Louis Fed, *Have You Met FRED?*](https://www.stlouisfed.org/timely-topics/have-you-met-fred); [Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, *FRED*](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/).
- **Limits of pure automation (revisions, discontinuities, and FRED-MD/FRED-QD):** St. Louis Fed economists Michael W. McCracken and Serena Ng documented that maintaining clean, consistent macroeconomic research panels (such as **FRED-MD** and **FRED-QD**) cannot rely solely on naive automation because upstream BEA, BLS, and Census releases undergo structural breaks, base-year changes, methodological shifts, and series discontinuations requiring explicit human judgment, historical splicing, and data-desk quality control — [Michael W. McCracken & Serena Ng, *"FRED-MD: A Monthly Database for Macroeconomic Research,"* Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis Working Paper 2015-012 (2015)](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/frbsl_wp/2015-012.pdf).
- **Bi-temporal vintage tracking via ALFRED:** The St. Louis Fed's companion system **ALFRED (ArchivaL Federal Reserve Economic Data)** stores historical data vintages using bi-temporal attributes (`observation_date`, `realtime_start`, `realtime_end`), capturing every BEA GDP revision (advance, second, third, annual, comprehensive) and BLS revision as it existed on any historical date, and exposing full vintage histories through the FRED API (`fred/series/observations`) — [St. Louis Fed, *What Is FRED / ALFRED*](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/help/about/about-fred/what-is-fred); [St. Louis Fed, *2018 Annual Report*](https://www.stlouisfed.org/news-releases/2019/04/16/st-louis-fed-2018-annual-report-go-figure-with-fred).
- **Official Federal Reserve System dissemination hub (2025–2027):** Under the Board–St. Louis Fed partnership, FRED has absorbed the Federal Reserve Board's Data Download Program (DDP) series (`https://fred.stlouisfed.org/collection/federal-reserve-board-data`), providing bulk release retrieval in JSON/XML via the FRED API plus CSV/Excel exports for both Board statistical releases and third-party BEA/BLS/Census indicators — [Federal Reserve Board, *DDP and FRED Partnership* (Nov. 12, 2026)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/data-download-fred-information.htm).

### Inferences
- FRED's ingestion architecture effectively serves as a System-wide public harmonization layer for the fragmented U.S. statistical system, solving the same cross-agency normalization problem publicly that Haver Analytics solves commercially.
- Maintaining ALFRED's bi-temporal vintage database requires release-day ingestion pipelines to diff incoming BEA/BLS payloads against prior stored observations rather than blindly overwriting series, which makes strict tracking of agency release calendars and revision schedules essential.

### Gaps
- **Non-public ingestion engineering details:** The St. Louis Fed does not publicly document the internal software stack, source-specific transport protocols (e.g., whether BEA NIPA ingestion currently hits `apps.bea.gov/api/data` vs. bulk files, or whether BLS CPI ingestion pulls from `download.bls.gov` flat files vs. API v2 vs. a bilateral government feed), or whether any formal interagency data-sharing agreements grant FRED priority access or whitelisted IP/rate-limit exemptions on release mornings.
- **Release-day latency metrics:** No official St. Louis Fed SLA or benchmark was found documenting exact average ingestion latency (seconds vs. minutes) between an 8:30 a.m. ET BEA/BLS public release and publication on FRED/ALFRED. (Note also that `fred.stlouisfed.org/help/*` pages returned HTTP 302 redirects during automated retrieval, limiting direct scraping of specific FRED Help FAQ text on update timing.)

## 4. OMB Statistical Policy Directive No. 3 and Pre-Release / Embargo Access Rules

### Takeaway
Under **OMB Statistical Policy Directive No. 3** (1985, updated effective February 19, 2024), standing pre-release access to Principal Federal Economic Indicators (PFEIs such as GDP and CPI) is explicitly granted **to the President through the Chair of the Council of Economic Advisers (CEA)**—and up to 30 minutes prior to parent-department policy officials (e.g., Commerce) in secure settings—whereas **no standing pre-release access to BEA/BLS PFEIs is codified for the Federal Reserve**, which relies on simultaneous public release and independent real-time monitoring.

### Cited Findings
- **Statutory/regulatory authority and core mandate of Directive No. 3:** Adopted by OMB OIRA on September 25, 1985 (`50 FR 38932`), **Statistical Policy Directive No. 3** (*Compilation, Release, and Evaluation of Principal Federal Economic Indicators*) governs PFEIs to preserve time value (targeting release within 22 working days of reference-period close), prevent premature disclosure that could move financial or commodity markets, mandate annual advance release schedules, require 3-report/3-month advance notice for methodological changes, mandate 3-year performance evaluations, and separate policy-neutral statistical release from policy interpretation — [OMB, *Statistical Policy Directive No. 3* (50 FR 38932, Sept. 25, 1985)](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2018-05/federalregister09251985.pdf).
- **Protection of public data banks prior to release:** Section 5 of Directive No. 3 explicitly directs that statistical agencies must prevent premature disclosure during report preparation, stating: *"This includes the protection of public use data banks, which shall not receive any data or estimates until they are officially released."* — [OMB, *Statistical Policy Directive No. 3*, Section 5 (50 FR 38933, Sept. 25, 1985)](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2018-05/federalregister09251985.pdf).
- **Standing pre-release access for the President via the CEA vs. strict 30-minute cap for others:** Section 5 of Directive No. 3 establishes a single standing institutional pre-release conduit outside the releasing statistical agency: *"The agency will provide prerelease information to the President, through the Chairman of the Council of Economic Advisers, as soon as it is available."* Any other pre-release access granted by an agency head under embargo is tightly capped: *(a)* strict security arrangements to prevent unauthorized dissemination/use, *(b)* explicit recipient agreement, *(c)* **"Any prerelease of information under an embargo shall not precede the official release time by more than 30 minutes,"** and *(d)* only to the extent necessary for orderly review — [OMB, *Statistical Policy Directive No. 3*, Section 5 (50 FR 38933, Sept. 25, 1985)](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2018-05/federalregister09251985.pdf).
- **BEA's current (December 2025) implementation of pre-release rules:** BEA's *Updated Security and Release Procedures* (December 2025) and *Data Dissemination Practices* page confirm that for BEA's five PFEIs (GDP, Personal Income and Outlays, Corporate Profits, U.S. International Trade in Goods and Services, International Transactions Accounts):
  1. Pre-release data is provided to **the President through the Chair of the Council of Economic Advisers** once estimates are finalized and final public release materials are prepared;
  2. No earlier than **30 minutes ahead** of official release time, the BEA Director may grant secure-environment pre-release access to the **Under Secretary for Economic Affairs and other Commerce Department policy officials** (with zero external communication permitted until public release);
  3. In "certain and extremely limited situations," the Director/Deputy Director may authorize logged, embargoed pre-release access to government policy officials or news media **no more than 30 minutes** before release.
  Neither BEA's Data Dissemination Practices nor its December 2025 Security and Release Procedures mentions any standing pre-release transfer to the Federal Reserve — [BEA, *Data Dissemination Practices* (last modified Dec. 19, 2025)](https://www.bea.gov/about/policies-and-information/data-dissemination); [BEA, *Updated Security and Release Procedures* (Dec. 2025)](https://www.bea.gov/system/files/2025-12/Security-and-Release-Procedures.pdf).
- **February 2024 OMB amendment to Directive No. 3 (commentary delay shortened to 30 minutes):** On February 15, 2024 (`89 FR 11873`, effective **February 19, 2024**), OMB updated Directive No. 3 by amending **solely** the Executive Branch commentary waiting period—replacing *"employees of the Executive Branch shall not comment publicly on the data until at least one hour after the official release time"* with **"thirty minutes"**—while leaving all pre-release security and CEA-access provisions unchanged — [OMB Notice, *Update of Statistical Policy Directive No. 3* (89 FR 11873, Feb. 15, 2024)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/02/15/2024-02972/update-of-statistical-policy-directive-no-3-compilation-release-and-evaluation-of-principal-federal); [OMB Prior Request for Comment (84 FR 14682, April 11, 2019)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/04/11/2019-07172/statistical-policy-directive-no-3-compilation-release-and-evaluation-of-principal-federal-economic).
- **Federal Reserve dependence on public release timing and recent BLS release glitches (2024–2025):** Because market-moving BLS releases (CPI, Employment Situation, benchmark payroll revisions) are consumed simultaneously at official release time (typically 8:30 a.m. ET), BLS release mishaps—such as the August 2024 delayed publication of preliminary payroll benchmark revisions where some callers obtained figures before the website updated—and federal funding/data disruptions have drawn intense scrutiny and directly affected Federal Reserve monitoring (for instance, the Cleveland Fed's Inflation Nowcasting had to substitute model nowcasts for missing October 2025 CPI data during a release disruption) — [Washington Post, *Technical error / BLS jobs delay* (Aug. 28, 2024)](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2024/08/28/technical-error-bls-jobs-delay/); [Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland, *Inflation Nowcasting*](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/inflation-nowcasting).

### Inferences
- The absence of the Federal Reserve from Section 5 of OMB Statistical Policy Directive No. 3 and from BEA's December 2025 Security and Release Procedures—combined with the Board's status as an independent institution outside the Executive Branch's cabinet hierarchy—indicates that the Federal Reserve receives BEA GDP and BLS CPI releases at **official public release time** (typically 8:30 a.m. ET) rather than receiving overnight/afternoon-before CEA-style pre-release briefings.
- This structural separation reinforces why Federal Reserve Banks invest heavily in real-time nowcasting models (e.g., Cleveland Fed Inflation Nowcasting, Philadelphia Fed ADS Index, NY Fed/Atlanta Fed trackers) and automated release-day ingestion pipelines rather than relying on privileged advance feeds.

### Gaps
- **BLS-specific internal pre-release distribution list:** `https://www.bls.gov/bls/statistical-policy-directive-3.pdf` and `https://www.bls.gov/bls/pre-release.htm` returned HTTP 403 during automated retrieval, preventing verbatim verification of BLS's internal pre-release list (e.g., confirming whether the Chair of the Federal Reserve ever receives discretionary ≤30-minute or CEA-relayed pre-release notice for BLS Employment Situation or CPI, or is strictly excluded like all non-Executive-Branch entities). General web summaries occasionally assert loosely that "designated central bank personnel" may receive lock-up access, which is **contradicted** by the verbatim text of OMB Directive No. 3 Section 5 and BEA's Dec. 2025 Security Procedures (which authorize only the President via the CEA Chair plus ≤30-minute Commerce/logged embargoed access); a direct check of BLS's current pre-release PDF or an official Fed/BLS FOIA disclosure would be needed to confirm with 100% certainty that zero informal or exceptional ≤30-minute pre-release channel exists for BLS series.

## 5. BIS IFC Papers, Modernization Efforts, and Industry Context on Central Bank Pipelines

### Takeaway
Recent **BIS Irving Fisher Committee (IFC)** work (2023–2026) documents a broad central bank transition away from legacy **FAME** time-series environments toward **Python/R**, SQL/NoSQL/lakehouse architectures, and **SDMX** interoperability—mirroring the Federal Reserve's own trajectory of adding Python to FRB/US (2022), modernizing metadata management, and retiring the Board's legacy DDP into FRED's API-first ecosystem (2025–2027).

### Cited Findings
- **BIS IFC "Future of Time Series" central bank survey (published July 2026):** In a BIS IFC survey of 19 central banks and statistical agencies presented at the IFC–Bank of Italy workshop (Oct. 2023) and published in *IFC Bulletin No. 64* (July 2026), Ryland Thomas (Bank of England) found that **12 of 19 surveyed institutions still use FAME**—of which **5 are actively transitioning away**—**2 have already transitioned fully off FAME**, **5 never used FAME** (relying on bespoke systems), and **zero non-FAME institutions plan to adopt FAME** — [Ryland Thomas (Bank of England), *"Future of time series: preliminary results from a BIS-IFC survey of central banks and statistical agencies,"* BIS IFC Bulletin No. 64, Paper 20 (July 2026)](https://www.bis.org/2026-07/ifcb64_20.pdf).
- **Strengths and pain points driving FAME modernization across central banks:** Surveyed central banks cited FAME's strong native time-series functionality, dynamic formulas, stability, Excel populator add-in, and deep entrenchment in legacy analytical pipelines as reasons for retention—while citing **declining Excel usage** in favor of **R, Python, and Tableau** (which integrate poorly with FAME), dwindling FAME engineering talent, high licensing costs, inability to handle large/unstructured microdata, and weak native metadata/searchability as drivers for migration. No single commercial replacement has emerged: migrating central banks are adopting SQL data warehouses, NoSQL, HBase, or cloud data lakehouses, while some are decoupling FAME's 4GL layer and pairing FAME's core database with open-source tools — [Ryland Thomas, BIS IFC Bulletin No. 64, Paper 20 (July 2026)](https://www.bis.org/2026-07/ifcb64_20.pdf).
- **SDMX adoption across central banks:** BIS IFC Report No. 4 (*Central banks' use of the SDMX standard*) tracks central banks' global standardization on ISO-standard **SDMX** for statistical exchange and dissemination—an initiative in which the Federal Reserve Board was an early pioneer when it launched SDMX-ML custom downloads in the Data Download Program in April 2006 — [BIS IFC Report No. 4, *Central banks' use of the SDMX standard*](https://www.bis.org/publications/ifc-report-4-central-banks-use-the-sdmx-standard); [Federal Reserve Board Press Release (April 19, 2006)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20060419a.htm).
- **Observed Federal Reserve modernization milestones (2022–2027):** Publicly verifiable Federal Reserve technical modernization aligns closely with these IFC themes:
  1. **Open-source modeling migration:** Adding a Board-built **Python** implementation of the flagship **FRB/US** macroeconomic model (April 2022) alongside legacy commercial EViews — [Federal Reserve Board, *The FRB/US Project*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-about.htm).
  2. **API-first dissemination consolidation:** Retiring the 20-year-old Board **Data Download Program (DDP)** between December 2025 and July 1, 2027 in favor of the St. Louis Fed's **FRED** platform (offering modern JSON/XML API bulk release endpoints, keyword search, interactive charting, and CSV/Excel downloads) while retaining XML release files on Board pages — [Federal Reserve Board, *DDP and FRED Partnership* (Nov. 12, 2026)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/data-download-fred-information.htm).
  3. **Relational metadata and real-time vintage tracking:** Transitioning internal operational metadata and release calendars from static Word/HTML files into SQL-backed repositories linked to real-time snapshot/vintage stores (Board FAME metadata repository; St. Louis Fed ALFRED) — [Cannon & Krug, IASSIST Quarterly (Spring 2006)](https://iassistquarterly.com/public/pdfs/iqvol301cannon.pdf); [St. Louis Fed, *What Is FRED / ALFRED*](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/help/about/about-fred/what-is-fred).

### Inferences
- Because the BIS IFC survey anonymized individual central bank responses in its public slides, the Federal Reserve Board's exact position among the "using FAME / transitioning away / fully off FAME" cohorts cannot be proven solely from `ifcb64_20.pdf`, though the Board's historical FAME investment (Cannon & Krug 2006; Hallman `fame` R package) and recent shift toward Python (FRB/US) and FRED API consolidation mirror the exact pressures documented across peer central banks.
- Retiring the Board's legacy SDMX-ML DDP web app into FRED reflects a pragmatic recognition that modern data consumers—including economists, financial firms, and internal researchers—increasingly prefer RESTful JSON/XML APIs (FRED API) and unified cross-agency aggregators over bespoke central-bank web portals.

### Gaps
- **No recent (2020–2026) Federal Reserve Board engineering post or BIS IFC paper detailing the Board's current internal cloud/data-lake ingestion architecture** was found in public sources. While Ryland Thomas's BIS IFC Bulletin 64 paper provides detailed anonymized cross-central-bank findings on FAME migration, specific public technical architecture write-ups authored by Federal Reserve Board IT/Research Data staff after 2006 (comparable to Bank of England or ECB data-platform blogs) are sparse or non-public.
- **Regional Reserve Bank heterogeneity:** The 12 regional Federal Reserve Banks operate semi-autonomous research IT infrastructures (e.g., St. Louis Fed's FRED, Cleveland Fed's Center for Inflation Research, Philadelphia Fed's Real-Time Data Research Center), and public documentation does not provide a consolidated inventory of how each Reserve Bank's internal ingestion stack differs from the Board of Governors' pipeline.
