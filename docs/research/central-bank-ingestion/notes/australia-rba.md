## 1. National Statistical Production and ABS Dissemination / API Channels

### Takeaway
The Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) is Australia's official producer of Gross Domestic Product (GDP), the Consumer Price Index (CPI), and Labour Force statistics, and by 2025–2026 operates a hybrid dissemination architecture combining downloadable Excel time-series spreadsheets and data cubes on `abs.gov.au`, a production-grade **ABS Indicator API** (launched live in February 2025), and **Beta**-designated **ABS Data API** and **ABS Data Explorer** services built on the SDMX 2.1 standard (replacing the retired ABS.Stat platform).

### Cited Findings
- The ABS produces Australia's headline macroeconomic series—including *Australian National Accounts: National Income, Expenditure and Product* (GDP, Cat. No. 5206.0), *Consumer Price Index, Australia* (CPI, Cat. No. 6401.0), *Labour Force, Australia* (Cat. No. 6202.0), and *Wage Price Index* (Cat. No. 6345.0)—releasing content simultaneously via `abs.gov.au` at **11:30 am Canberra time** on release day — [ABS Policy on Pre-Release Access to ABS Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics); [RBA Chart Pack Data Availability](https://www.rba.gov.au/chart-pack/data-availability.html).
- By late 2025 / early 2026, the ABS introduced a **complete monthly Consumer Price Index (CPI)** (replacing the earlier quarterly-only headline CPI plus monthly CPI indicator structure), and also added the **Monthly Household Spending Indicator** to its core monthly release calendar — [ABS Policy on Pre-Release Access to ABS Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics); [RBA Changes to Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/changes-to-tables.html).
- **ABS Indicator API (Production, Live as of 20 February 2025):** The ABS launched a dedicated production REST API (`indicator.api.abs.gov.au`) specifically for fast, machine-to-machine retrieval of headline economic statistics **as soon as the embargo is lifted at 11:30 am Canberra time**. It uses the SDMX standard (supporting XML, JSON, and CSV), restricts datasets to small, high-demand headline tables for low latency, covers GDP (*Australian National Accounts, Expenditure on GDP*), CPI, Monthly CPI indicator, Labour Force, Average Weekly Earnings, Balance of Payments, Business Indicators, Building Approvals, Construction Work Done, International Trade, and Job Vacancies, and **requires an API key** obtained by emailing a signed request form to `api.data@abs.gov.au` — [ABS Indicator API](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/application-programming-interfaces-apis/indicator-api).
- **ABS Data API (Beta):** For full, multi-dimensional economic, social, and Census datasets, the ABS operates an unauthenticated SDMX 2.1-compliant REST API at `https://data.api.abs.gov.au/rest/` returning **SDMX-ML (XML)**, **SDMX-JSON**, and **SDMX-CSV**, supporting dataflow metadata discovery (`/rest/dataflow`) and dimensional filtering (`/rest/data/{agencyId},{dataflowId},{version}/{dataKey}`). The ABS explicitly warns that the Data API remains in **Beta**: availability is not guaranteed, performance may degrade under high load, datasets are updated "as soon as possible after embargo" rather than guaranteed at 11:30 am sharp, and a major system update on **29 November 2024** altered how users access and request data — [ABS Data API User Guide](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/application-programming-interfaces-apis/data-api-user-guide); [ABS Understanding SDMX Data](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/application-programming-interfaces-apis/data-api-user-guide/understanding-sdmx-data).
- **ABS Data Explorer (Beta) and retirement of ABS.Stat:** The legacy **ABS.Stat** web portal has been retired and replaced by **ABS Data Explorer** (`https://dataexplorer.abs.gov.au`), an interactive `.Stat` browser interface (still designated Beta in ABS user documentation) for filtering, charting, exporting Excel/CSV, and auto-generating SDMX API calls via a "Developer API" tab. Like the Data API, the ABS warns that Data Explorer datasets are loaded "as soon as possible after their 11:30am release" and may lag the primary `abs.gov.au` website — [ABS Data Explorer User Guide](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/application-programming-interfaces-apis/data-explorer-user-guide).
- **Downloadable Excel time-series spreadsheets and data cubes remain primary website deliverables:** Alongside the SDMX APIs, the ABS continues to publish structured Excel time-series spreadsheets (indexed by ABS Catalogue Numbers and Series IDs) and non-standard Excel "data cubes" directly on `abs.gov.au`. This dual-channel reality is reflected in Australia's leading open-source R package `{readabs}` (maintained by Matt Cowgill), which maintains three separate ingestion paths: `read_abs()` specifically for standard ABS Excel time-series spreadsheets, `download_abs_data_cube()` for non-standard Excel cubes, and `read_api()` for the SDMX Data API — [readabs GitHub README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MattCowgill/readabs/master/README.md).
- **ABS upstream modernization:** Internally, the ABS has pursued a multi-year **Data Acquisition Modernisation Program** and cloud/big-data transition (*Big Data, Timely Insights*) to modernize legacy collection and production systems, alongside tripartite coordination with the RBA and APRA on the Economic and Financial Statistics (EFS) collection to eliminate duplicate business reporting — [ABS Statistics & APIs Portal](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/application-programming-interfaces-apis).

### Inferences
- The ABS's creation of a **two-tier API architecture**—separating a live, key-gated, low-latency **Indicator API** (guaranteed at 11:30 am embargo lift) from the full **Data API (Beta)** (no SLA, post-embargo loading lag)—is a direct engineering response to the institutional needs of financial markets, commercial data vendors, and policy agencies that cannot tolerate Beta latency or downtime on CPI/GDP release days.
- Because the full ABS Data API remains labeled Beta and warns that `abs.gov.au` is authoritative for the latest data, downloadable Excel time-series spreadsheets have not been sunset and continue to serve as a fallback or primary source for detailed sub-components.

### Gaps
- The exact decommissioning date of legacy **AusStats** and **ABS.Stat** portals was not confirmed from a primary ABS deprecation notice in the fetched pages (though ABS.Stat's retirement in favor of Data Explorer is documented).
- Rate limits, SLA uptime figures, and whether government agencies like the RBA hold privileged API keys or dedicated endpoints on `indicator.api.abs.gov.au` or `data.api.abs.gov.au` are not publicly documented.

## 2. RBA Data Ingestion Infrastructure, Vendors, and Analytical Stack

### Takeaway
Public procurement and RBA website disclosures confirm that the RBA operates a centralized **Enterprise Data Platform (EDP)** combining IBM InfoSphere, Cloudera, Azure SQL MI, Posit (R/Python), Tableau, and Power BI, alongside commercial data vendors (notably **LSEG/Refinitiv**, **CEIC Data**, **Bloomberg**, **Markit Economics**, and property data providers **Cotality/CoreLogic, APM, Residex**) for international, market, and non-ABS series; however, the RBA does **not** publicly document the exact internal ingestion mechanism it uses to pull ABS macroeconomic releases (API vs. Excel parsing vs. pre-release file transfer vs. vendor feed).

### Cited Findings
- **RBA Enterprise Data Platform (EDP) technology stack:** In a February 2025 Request for Proposal (RFP) for a 36-month managed service provider contract (plus two 12-month extension options) to maintain its centralized **Enterprise Data Platform (EDP)**, the RBA disclosed a core enterprise data stack comprising: **IBM InfoSphere Information Server** (ETL/data integration), **Cloudera Data Platform and Cloudera Machine Learning**, **Microsoft Azure SQL Managed Instance (SQL MI)**, **Posit** (commercial R/Python data science suite), **Microsoft Power BI**, and **Tableau** — [techpartner.news (11 Feb 2025)](https://www.techpartner.news/news/rba-seeks-managed-service-provider-for-enterprise-data-platform-614931).
- **Commercial data aggregators confirmed in RBA publications:** The RBA's *Chart Pack Data Availability* documentation explicitly identifies commercial third-party data vendors whose feeds power RBA analytical charts and cannot be freely redistributed due to contractual licensing restrictions:
  - **LSEG (formerly Refinitiv):** Used extensively for international macroeconomic series (Major Trading Partner GDP, G3 GDP, G3 CPI, G3 Labour Markets, East Asia CPI) — [RBA Chart Pack Data Availability](https://www.rba.gov.au/chart-pack/data-availability.html).
  - **CEIC Data:** Used for China, India, East Asia, and G3 macroeconomic indicators (GDP, CPI, labour markets, China activity, Total Social Financing, residential property) — [RBA Chart Pack Data Availability](https://www.rba.gov.au/chart-pack/data-availability.html).
  - **Markit Economics (S&P Global PMI), ANZ-Roy Morgan, Westpac–Melbourne Institute, NAB Business Survey, Cotality (CoreLogic), APM, and Residex:** Used for sentiment, business conditions, and housing price indicators — [RBA Chart Pack Data Availability](https://www.rba.gov.au/chart-pack/data-availability.html).
  - **Bloomberg and LSEG/Refinitiv:** Used in market operations and benchmark distribution (e.g., Cash Rate Total Return Index) — [RBA Statistics Portal](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/).
- **Direct ABS attribution for domestic macro data:** In contrast to international macro charts (sourced via LSEG/CEIC), the RBA's *Chart Pack Data Availability* attributes every domestic Australian GDP, State Final Demand, CPI, underlying inflation, household income/consumption, housing debt, and business investment series directly to **ABS Catalogue Numbers** (e.g., 5206.0, 6401.0, 5232.0, 5625.0, 8731.0) and corresponding RBA Statistical Tables (G1, H1, H2) — [RBA Chart Pack Data Availability](https://www.rba.gov.au/chart-pack/data-availability.html).
- **Microdata collaboration with ABS:** Beyond aggregate macro releases, RBA researchers access linked Australian microdata environments hosted/developed with the ABS—such as the **Business Longitudinal Analysis Data Environment (BLADE)** and pilot datasets like the **Wealth and Housing Assets Module (WHAM)**—and co-host an annual **ABS and RBA Joint Conference** (2022–2026 themes covering digital economy, price change, human capital, housing data/analytics, and migration) — [ABS and RBA Joint Conferences](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/other-confs/abs-and-rba-joint-conferences/).
- **APRA / Economic and Financial Statistics (EFS) collection:** Financial-sector balance sheet and lending statistics feeding RBA Financial Aggregates are collected jointly via APRA's reporting infrastructure; as of 2026, the RBA's Statistical Tables portal explicitly warns of potential publication delays resulting from the **decommissioning of the Direct to APRA (D2A) reporting system** for financial institutions — [RBA Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/).
- **R/Python open-source ecosystem:** While the RBA's EDP explicitly includes **Posit** (supporting R and Python workflows), external open-source packages (`readabs` and `readrba`, both authored by Matt Cowgill) were built primarily for external researchers to parse ABS Excel spreadsheets/APIs and RBA Excel/CSV tables into tidy R tibbles — [techpartner.news (11 Feb 2025)](https://www.techpartner.news/news/rba-seeks-managed-service-provider-for-enterprise-data-platform-614931); [readabs GitHub README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MattCowgill/readabs/master/README.md); [readrba GitHub README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MattCowgill/readrba/master/README.md).

### Inferences
- The presence of **IBM InfoSphere Information Server** (enterprise ETL), **Azure SQL MI**, **Cloudera**, and **Posit** in the RBA's EDP strongly indicates that RBA economists run programmatic ETL and R/Python analytical pipelines rather than relying purely on manual Excel copy-pasting, even though public outputs remain Excel/CSV.
- The clear split in *Chart Pack Data Availability*—where international macro data relies on commercial aggregators (LSEG, CEIC) while domestic macro data cites ABS Catalogue Numbers directly—suggests the RBA ingests core Australian ABS releases directly from the ABS (and/or via internal ETL from ABS dissemination channels) rather than solely re-purchasing domestic ABS series via LSEG/CEIC.

### Gaps
- **No public confirmation of internal ABS ingestion plumbing:** Publicly available RBA documents do not specify whether internal RBA pipelines ingest ABS GDP/CPI/Labour Force data via the **ABS Indicator API**, the **ABS Data API (SDMX)**, automated downloads of **ABS Excel time-series spreadsheets**, or private government-to-government file transfers.
- **No public confirmation of FAME, Haver Analytics, or Macrobond at the RBA:** Targeted searches of `rba.gov.au` and `tenders.gov.au` (AusTender) returned **no public records** confirming whether the RBA currently uses or previously retired **FIS/SunGard FAME**, **Haver Analytics**, or **Macrobond** for macroeconomic time-series storage. (Note: AusTender public search snippets did not surface specific vendor contract awards under those brand names, likely because subscriptions may be procured via panels, multi-year master agreements, or under generic "market/economic data" descriptions.)
- Internal data-loading mechanics for the RBA's macroeconometric model (**MARTIN**) and whether RBA analysts use `readabs` internally on Posit are not publicly documented.

## 3. ABS Pre-Release and Embargo Arrangements with the RBA and Treasury

### Takeaway
The ABS maintains a strict simultaneous-release policy at **11:30 am Canberra time**, granting embargoed pre-release access solely through ABS-managed physical/secure **"lock-ups"** for nine specified market-sensitive economic releases (including the now-monthly CPI, quarterly GDP, and monthly Labour Force), plus an explicit **day-before pre-release privilege for approved Commonwealth Treasury officials for National Accounts (GDP)**—a day-before privilege that the published ABS policy does **not** list for the RBA.

### Cited Findings
- **Core simultaneous release rule:** The ABS *Policy on Pre-Release Access to ABS Statistics* (updated **20 October 2026**) establishes equality of access via simultaneous release on `abs.gov.au` at **11:30 am Canberra time**; prior to release time, all ABS statistics are strictly confidential and under embargo — [ABS Policy on Pre-Release Access to ABS Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics).
- **Criteria and purpose of pre-release access:** Embargoed pre-release access is restricted to selected government stakeholders where either (a) interpretation of content is complex, or (b) an Australian government minister, official, or agency is likely to provide public comment on release day. The policy explicitly states that the ABS "does not provide pre-release access to seek comments on these finalised statistics and publications" — [ABS Policy on Pre-Release Access to ABS Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics).
- **ABS-hosted secure lock-ups:** For market-sensitive releases, authorised government officials attend a secure room managed by ABS staff, must sign a binding **security undertaking with legal penalties for breaches**, and are strictly prohibited from communicating outside the room until the 11:30 am embargo lifts. As of October 2026, ABS-hosted lock-ups are approved for **nine** releases:
  1. *Balance of Payments and International Investment Position, Australia* (quarterly)
  2. *Labour Force, Australia* (monthly), with *Average Weekly Earnings* (half yearly)
  3. *Consumer Price Index, Australia* (**monthly** — reflecting the transition to full monthly CPI)
  4. *Australian National Accounts: National Income, Expenditure and Product* (quarterly — GDP)
  5. *Private New Capital Expenditure and Expected Expenditure, Australia* (quarterly)
  6. *Wage Price Index, Australia* (quarterly)
  7. *Construction Work Done, Australia, Preliminary* (quarterly)
  8. *Building Approvals, Australia* (monthly)
  9. *Monthly Household Spending Indicator* (monthly) — [ABS Policy on Pre-Release Access to ABS Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics).
- **Day-before GDP access exclusively noted for Commonwealth Treasury:** The October 2026 ABS policy states: *"Approved Commonwealth Treasury officials are also granted access to Australian National Accounts: National Income, Expenditure and Product the day before its release."* Ad hoc lock-ups for other releases require exceptional-circumstances approval — [ABS Policy on Pre-Release Access to ABS Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics).
- **Machine-to-machine post-embargo delivery as complement to lock-ups:** The ABS pre-release policy explicitly pairs its lock-up section with the **ABS Indicator API**, noting that *"In addition to secure lock-ups, the ABS Indicator Application Programming Interface (API) provides key economic data as soon as embargo is lifted at 11.30 am (Canberra time)"* — [ABS Policy on Pre-Release Access to ABS Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics).
- **RBA's own media lock-ups:** Separate from ABS releases, the RBA conducts its own embargoed media lock-ups (governed by signed deed polls) for major RBA policy publications such as Board Minutes, the *Statement on Monetary Policy*, and the *Financial Stability Review* — [RBA Statistics Portal](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/).

### Inferences
- Because the ABS pre-release policy strictly confines lock-up attendees to a no-communication ABS room until 11:30 am—and names Commonwealth Treasury (not the RBA) for day-before GDP access—any automated ingestion of ABS CPI or GDP into RBA internal databases, forecasting models, and Statistical Tables cannot be transmitted outside an ABS lock-up prior to 11:30 am on release day.
- Adding the **ABS Indicator API** reference directly into the ABS Pre-Release Access Policy signals that the ABS views instantaneous 11:30 am API delivery as the primary high-speed channel for non-lock-up (and post-embargo automated) institutional consumption.

### Gaps
- The October 2026 ABS policy lists approved *releases* for lock-ups and explicitly names Commonwealth Treasury for day-before GDP access, but does **not** publish the exact roster of attending agencies (e.g., whether RBA officials currently attend every ABS lock-up or only select releases) or exact lock-up lead times (e.g., 1 hour vs. 2 hours before 11:30 am).
- Historical documentation of post-2014 ABS pre-release access policy reviews (following the 2014 ABS data-leak insider trading case) and whether ABS lock-ups shifted temporarily or permanently to virtual/electronic secure transfers during or after COVID-19 was not verified from primary ABS review reports in this research pass.

## 4. RBA Public Disclosures on Data Governance, Modernization, and BIS IFC Engagement

### Takeaway
Across its 2024–2025 Annual Reports, February 2025 EDP procurement, and international leadership in the BIS Irving Fisher Committee (IFC)—chaired by RBA Governor Michele Bullock—the RBA has publicly documented major organizational and infrastructure modernization (**CoreMod**, restructuring the Enterprise Data Office into the Information Department, and outsourcing EDP managed services), while keeping internal statistical ETL pipelines and vendor-specific macroeconomic database choices largely undisclosed.

### Cited Findings
- **Core Modernisation Program (CoreMod):** Initiated in 2024 under the Enterprise Services and Strategy Group (overseen by the Chief Operating Officer), the RBA's multi-year **CoreMod** program upgrades aging core technology infrastructure, establishes a modern co-location data centre environment (partnering with CDC Data Centres), and migrates critical application workloads to mitigate obsolescence risks — [RBA Annual Report 2024](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/annual-reports/rba/2024/); [RBA Annual Report 2025](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/annual-reports/rba/2025/).
- **Organizational data governance shift:** In its 2024 Annual Report cycle, the RBA restructured its **Enterprise Data Office** into the **Information Department**, broadening its mandate across enterprise data governance and knowledge management — [RBA Annual Report 2024](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/annual-reports/rba/2024/).
- **Enterprise Data Platform (EDP) managed services RFP (February 2025):** The RBA publicly tendered for a managed service provider to operate its EDP (IBM InfoSphere, Cloudera Data Platform/ML, Azure SQL MI, Power BI, Posit, Tableau) with explicit statutory requirements around configuration tracking (asset register integration), data location, and privacy compliance — [techpartner.news (11 Feb 2025)](https://www.techpartner.news/news/rba-seeks-managed-service-provider-for-enterprise-data-platform-614931).
- **Governance Board commencement (1 March 2025):** Following the RBA Review reforms, the new RBA Governance Board commenced on 1 March 2025 (comprising the Governor, Deputy Governor, Chief Operating Officer, and six external members) to oversee organisational affairs, technology modernization, and operational infrastructure — [RBA Annual Report 2025](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/annual-reports/rba/2025/).
- **BIS Irving Fisher Committee (IFC) leadership and publications:** RBA Governor **Michele Bullock** serves as **Chair of the IFC Executive** at the Bank for International Settlements (BIS). In her opening remarks to the **13th Biennial IFC Conference** on **20 August 2026**, Bullock emphasized how dramatically tools for data construction and analysis have evolved alongside big data and new data sources, while underscoring that statistics must be paired with economic frameworks and judgement. RBA staff and case studies also contribute to BIS IFC Bulletins on central bank statistics, external statistics, data science, and AI (e.g., IFC Bulletins No. 52 and No. 67) — [Michele Bullock, Opening Remarks – 13th IFC Conference (20 Aug 2026)](https://www.rba.gov.au/speeches/2026/sp-gov-2026-08-21.html); [BIS IFC Bulletin No. 67 Overview (Apr 2026)](https://www.bis.org/2026-04/overview_ifc-bulletin-67_rev_.pdf); [BIS IFC Bulletin No. 52](https://www.bis.org/publications/ifc-bulletin-52-bridging-measurement-challenges-and-analytical-needs-external-statistics-evolution-or-revolution.pdf).
- **RBA inbound APIs exist for financial reporting, not public statistical dissemination:** The RBA has publicly documented secure API and XML submission gateways for regulated financial institutions—such as the **RBA Securitisation System** (supporting secure API/XML submission of loan-level and pool-level data) and banking/payment API Gateways (`RBAnet`/B2B)—demonstrating mature institutional API capability on inbound data collection — [RBA Statistics Portal](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/).
- **Freedom of Information (FOI) scope and statutory exemptions:** While the RBA maintains a public FOI Disclosure Log under the *Freedom of Information Act 1982*, the RBA is legally exempt from FOI regarding banking operations, exchange control, and protected information governed by secrecy provisions of the *Reserve Bank Act 1959* — [RBA Statistics & Governance](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/).

### Inferences
- The RBA's public disclosures focus heavily on enterprise-wide infrastructure resilience (**CoreMod**, data centre migration, **EDP** managed services) and macro-level statistical governance (IFC leadership, joint ABS conferences, microdata access like BLADE/WHAM), rather than granular technical documentation of how its Economic Analysis or Economic Research departments ingest ABS CSV/Excel/SDMX feeds.
- The absence of public FOI releases or AusTender notices specifically describing an "ABS API vs. Excel migration" indicates that ABS data ingestion changes are treated as routine internal IT/analytics operations rather than standalone public procurement or policy initiatives.

### Gaps
- Full-text extraction of the 2024 and 2025 RBA Annual Report PDFs (`rba-annual-report-2024.pdf`, `rba-annual-report-2025.pdf`) and BIS IFC Bulletin No. 67 PDF was not completed during this pass due to binary PDF sizes, so specific internal project names or RBA-authored technical papers inside IFC Bulletin No. 67 may contain additional details not captured here.
- No specific RBA FOI disclosure documents addressing ABS data feeds, FAME replacement, or commercial economic data contracts (Haver, Macrobond, Bloomberg, LSEG) were surfaced via web search.

## 5. RBA Handling of ABS Revisions, Metadata, and Re-Dissemination in Statistical Tables

### Takeaway
Despite the ABS's shift toward SDMX APIs, the RBA's own public **Statistical Tables** (including **G1**, **G4**, and **H1**) remain disseminated exclusively as downloadable **CSV and Excel files** (234 CSV links, 97 Excel links, and zero public API or SDMX endpoints as of November 2026), with explicit RBA Series IDs, source attribution (`ABS` vs. `ABS / RBA` vs. `RBA`), inline publication-date stamps, and a dedicated *Changes to Statistical Tables* changelog + RSS feed documenting ABS-driven methodological breaks and new tables.

### Cited Findings
- **Excel and CSV-only public dissemination (no RBA public API / SDMX):** Direct inspection of the live RBA Statistical Tables index (`https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/`, November 2026) shows **234 downloadable CSV links**, **97 Excel (`.xls`/`.xlsx`) links**, and **0 references to a public API or SDMX feed**. External programmatic access relies on downloading these static CSV/Excel files (e.g., via R's `{readrba}` package) or third-party cloud redistributors (such as Snowflake Marketplace syndications) — [RBA Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/); [readrba GitHub README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MattCowgill/readrba/master/README.md).
- **Table G1 (Consumer Price Inflation) metadata & value-added RBA calculations:** Direct inspection of `g1-data.csv` shows standardized header metadata rows (`Title`, `Description`, `Frequency`, `Type`, `Units`, `Source`, `Publication date`, `Series ID`). Notably, **G1 does not merely mirror raw ABS CPI tables**:
  - Headline and underlying series (`GCPIAG`, `GCPIAGYP`, trimmed mean `GCPIOCPMTMYP`, weighted median `GCPIOCPMWMYP`) carry `Source: ABS / RBA`.
  - Analytical decompositions—such as excluding volatile items (`GCPIXVIYP`), tradables (`GCPITIYP`), tradables excluding volatile items (`GCPITXVIYP`), and non-tradables (`GCPINTIYP`, `GCPINTXDLYP`)—carry `Source: RBA`, reflecting internal RBA transformations of ABS CPI components.
  - Historical series in `g1-data.csv` splice ABS data back to **June 1922** (`30/06/1922`), with a `Publication date` stamp of `29-Oct-2026` — [RBA Statistical Table G1 CSV](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/g1-data.csv).
- **Table H1 (Gross Domestic Product and Income) metadata & release cadence:** Direct inspection of `h1-data.csv` shows 11 quarterly seasonally adjusted series (Real GDP `GGDPCVGDP`, year-ended growth `GGDPCVGDPY`, non-farm GDP `GGDPCVGDPNF`, farm GDP `GGDPCVGDPF`, GDP per capita growth `GGDPCVGDPFY`, Real GDI `GGDPCVRGDI`, Nominal GDP `GGDPECCPGDP`, Terms of Trade `GOPITT`) extending back to **September 1959** (`30/09/1959`). Every column carries `Source: ABS` and a `Publication date` stamp (`03-Sep-2026`, updated promptly following the quarterly ABS National Accounts release) — [RBA Statistical Table H1 CSV](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/h1-data.csv).
- **Adaptation to ABS's 2025–2026 complete Monthly CPI (new Table G4):** On **29 January 2026**, the RBA announced via *Changes to Statistical Tables* the creation of a brand-new table—**Consumer Price Inflation – Monthly Collection – G4**—explicitly *"following the introduction of the complete monthly CPI by the ABS"*, updated monthly following each ABS CPI release — [RBA Changes to Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/changes-to-tables.html).
- **Handling of ABS revisions, re-referencing, and methodological breaks:**
  - The RBA prefaces its Statistical Tables portal with a standing disclaimer that tables *"are subject to revisions and may be withdrawn or discontinued at any time (in whole or in part)"*, routing users to the **Changes to Statistical Tables** page and its **RSS feed** — [RBA Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/); [RBA Changes to Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/changes-to-tables.html).
  - When the ABS alters methodologies or suspends series, the RBA propagates and documents the change explicitly with ABS citations—for example, reflecting the **ABS Labour Force 6202.0 (April 2020) Suspension of Trend Series** in RBA Table H5 due to COVID-19 time-series shocks — [RBA Changes to Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/changes-to-tables.html).
  - The RBA distinguishes **re-referencing/rounding** from substantive **revisions**: when the ABS changed the CPI reference period from 1989–90 to 2011–12 (October 2012, Table G2), the RBA explicitly noted that minor percentage-change shifts due to re-referencing and rounding *"do not constitute a revision"* — [RBA Changes to Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/changes-to-tables.html).
  - Long-horizon archives and discontinued tables are separated into a dedicated **Historical Data** portal (`historical-data.html`), while RBA's **Historical Forecasts** tables track past RBA forecasts alongside outcomes and forecast errors — [RBA Historical Data](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/historical-data.html); [RBA Statistics Portal](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/).

### Inferences
- Because RBA Statistical Tables like G1 and H1 preserve **proprietary RBA Series IDs** (e.g., `GCPIAG`, `GGDPCVGDP`), splice multi-decade historical series back to 1922/1959, and embed RBA-derived analytical series (tradables/non-tradables, trimmed mean, weighted median) alongside raw ABS figures, the RBA maintains a curated internal mapping layer between ABS source series and RBA table identifiers rather than acting as a passive pass-through mirror of ABS SDMX dataflows.
- The complete absence of an RBA public API or SDMX endpoint as of late 2026 contrasts sharply with the ABS's SDMX-REST Indicator API and Data API, making the RBA's public statistical dissemination comparatively traditional (static CSV/Excel files + RSS changelog) even while its internal Enterprise Data Platform has modernized.

### Gaps
- Exact intra-day lag between the ABS's 11:30 am Canberra release and the RBA's update of Tables G1, G4, and H1 (e.g., same-day afternoon vs. next business day—note `h1-data.csv` was stamped `03-Sep-2026`, one day after the typical first-Wednesday-of-September ABS Q2 GDP release) is not formally specified in an RBA SLA.
- Whether the RBA maintains an internal **real-time vintage database** (archiving every historical ABS revision vintage for backtesting MARTIN forecasts) and whether the RBA has any internal roadmap to launch a public SDMX/REST API for its Statistical Tables are not publicly disclosed.
