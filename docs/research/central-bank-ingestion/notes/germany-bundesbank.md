## 1. Who produces Germany's GDP and CPI/HICP, and how does the legal/institutional framework between Destatis, Deutsche Bundesbank, Eurostat, and the ECB work?

### Takeaway
Destatis (Statistisches Bundesamt) is the sole producer of Germany's official non-financial national accounts (including GDP), national CPI, and German HICP, while Deutsche Bundesbank compiles Germany's financial accounts and balance of payments and acts as a key user and seasonal-adjustment partner. Their collaboration operates across two legally distinct but closely linked pillars—the European Statistical System (ESS) and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB)—governed by German federal statutes (BStatG, BBankG), EU statistical regulations, a bilateral Bundesbank–Destatis Memorandum of Understanding (revised September 2021), and an updated ESS–ESCB Memorandum of Understanding (signed November 2024).

### Cited Findings
- **Division of national statistical production:** Destatis compiles Germany's real-economy national accounts (GDP, production, income, expenditure, and non-financial assets) as well as Germany's national Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP), while Deutsche Bundesbank compiles the quarterly financial accounts (*Finanzierungsrechnung*) under ESA 2010 and transmits them to the ECB (for euro area aggregates) and Eurostat (for EU aggregates) — [Destatis: National Accounts, Domestic Product](https://www.destatis.de/EN/Themes/Economy/National-Accounts-Domestic-Product/_node.html); [Deutsche Bundesbank: Financial Accounts](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/statistics/macroeconomic-accounting-systems/financial-accounts/financial-accounts-792676); [Deutsche Bundesbank: Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/statistics/economic-activity-and-prices/harmonised-consumer-prices/harmonised-index-of-consumer-prices-932146).
- **HICP vs. national CPI distinction:** Destatis calculates German HICP monthly using data from the national CPI under harmonised EU concepts; the main methodological difference is that German HICP excludes owner-occupied housing costs, giving services a smaller weight than in the national CPI. Bundesbank publishes inflation updates citing "Federal Statistical Office, Eurostat, Bundesbank calculation" — [Deutsche Bundesbank: Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/statistics/economic-activity-and-prices/harmonised-consumer-prices/harmonised-index-of-consumer-prices-932146).
- **German statutory foundations:** Destatis executes federal statistical tasks under **Section 3 of the Federal Statistics Act (*Bundesstatistikgesetz*, BStatG)**, while Deutsche Bundesbank executes central bank statistical tasks under **Section 18 of the Bundesbank Act (*Bundesbankgesetz*, BBankG)**, alongside statistical confidentiality and inter-agency register/data-sharing provisions in the BStatG and the Statistics Register Act (*Statistikregistergesetz*) — [Memorandum of Understanding between Deutsche Bundesbank and Federal Statistical Office, 13 September 2021](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/622452/607928e1af0a56a03c620202e65b5e65/mL/cooperation-agreement-between-the-bundesbank-data.pdf).
- **EU dual-pillar legal framework:** European statistics are governed by two separate legal frameworks: the **ESS** (Eurostat and EU NSIs, including Destatis) operates under **Regulation (EC) No 223/2009** on European statistics, whereas the **ESCB** (ECB and NCBs, including Deutsche Bundesbank) operates under **Article 5 of Protocol (No 4) on the Statute of the ESCB and ECB** and **Council Regulation (EC) No 2533/98**. Both **Article 9 of Regulation (EC) No 223/2009** and **Article 2a of Regulation (EC) No 2533/98** legally mandate close ESS–ESCB cooperation to guarantee data quality, coherence, and reduced reporting burden — [Memorandum of Understanding on Statistical Cooperation between the ESS and the ESCB, 20 November 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/13019146/21116249/memorandum-understanding-ess_escb.pdf/4db8d61c-741c-8797-aa29-b563c5c2dcf2?t=1740567304257); [Memorandum of Understanding between Deutsche Bundesbank and Federal Statistical Office, 13 September 2021](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/622452/607928e1af0a56a03c620202e65b5e65/mL/cooperation-agreement-between-the-bundesbank-data.pdf).
- **Bilateral Bundesbank–Destatis Memorandum of Understanding (2014, revised 2021):** First formalized in 2014 and last revised and signed on **13 September 2021** (unlimited duration, reviewed at least every five years), the bilateral MoU defines shared-responsibility domains in Section 1(2): national accounts and financial accounts, government finance/EDP statistics, foreign trade and balance of payments, GNI harmonisation, FATS, multinational enterprise groups / EuroGroups Register (EGR), enterprise master-data registers, institutional sector classification, **seasonal adjustment**, and Macroeconomic Imbalance Procedure (MIP) statistics — [Destatis: Deutsche Bundesbank Memorandum of Understanding](https://www.destatis.de/EN/About-Us/Our-Mission/bundesbank-memorandum.html); [Memorandum of Understanding between Deutsche Bundesbank and Federal Statistical Office, 13 September 2021](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/622452/607928e1af0a56a03c620202e65b5e65/mL/cooperation-agreement-between-the-bundesbank-data.pdf).
- **Mutual data provision and Bundesbank's privileged user role:** Under Section 2(1) of the 2021 bilateral MoU, Destatis and Bundesbank provide each other with required statistical data and metadata on agreed schedules, free of charge (Section 7), with annual management-level meetings and ongoing working-level exchange (Section 3). **Section 4 ("The Deutsche Bundesbank's special interest as a user")** explicitly establishes that information exchanges must account for the Bundesbank's strong user interest in federal statistics on the **real economy and prices** for monetary policy and financial stability analysis, without affecting Destatis's independent statistical responsibility — [Memorandum of Understanding between Deutsche Bundesbank and Federal Statistical Office, 13 September 2021](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/622452/607928e1af0a56a03c620202e65b5e65/mL/cooperation-agreement-between-the-bundesbank-data.pdf).
- **Updated ESS–ESCB Memorandum of Understanding (20 November 2024):** Signed in Frankfurt am Main by Mariana Kotzeva (Director-General of Eurostat / ESSC Chair) and Claudia Mann (Director General of ECB DG Statistics, as authorised nominee of ECB President Christine Lagarde), this MoU replaced the earlier **22 April 2013** ESS–ESCB MoU. It explicitly notes that the revised Regulation (EC) No 223/2009 and planned revision of Regulation (EC) No 2533/98 open new opportunities for ESS–ESCB data sharing for statistical purposes. Its Annex designates shared-responsibility/common-interest domains including national accounts (incl. GFS, except EDP), balance of payments, trade, FDI/FATS, business registers, climate-change statistics, and horizontal areas such as **seasonal adjustment**, revision policies, common technical solutions, and **coordination and improvement of confidential and non-confidential data exchange** — [Memorandum of Understanding on Statistical Cooperation between the ESS and the ESCB, 20 November 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/13019146/21116249/memorandum-understanding-ess_escb.pdf/4db8d61c-741c-8797-aa29-b563c5c2dcf2?t=1740567304257).
- **Governance bodies (ESF and CMFB):** Strategic ESS–ESCB cooperation is steered by the **European Statistical Forum (ESF)** (convened at least every three years, co-chaired by the Directors-General of Eurostat and ECB DG Statistics, and supported by the **ESF Bureau** meeting at least annually). Operational cooperation is executed through the **Committee on Monetary, Financial and Balance of Payments Statistics (CMFB)** (established under Council Decision 91/115/EEC and Council Decision 2006/856/EC), which presents biennial work programmes to the ESSC and ESCB Statistics Committee (STC) — [Memorandum of Understanding on Statistical Cooperation between the ESS and the ESCB, 20 November 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/13019146/21116249/memorandum-understanding-ess_escb.pdf/4db8d61c-741c-8797-aa29-b563c5c2dcf2?t=1740567304257); [ECB Economic Bulletin, Issue 7 / 2015, Article 3](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/eb201507_article03.en.pdf).

### Inferences
- Because the bilateral MoU is non-binding (Section 6 explicitly states it creates no legal liability) and rests on statutory complementarity (BStatG § 3 vs. BBankG § 18), German macroeconomic data sharing relies heavily on institutionalized working-level bilateral agreements (Section 1(4) supplementary agreements) combined with binding EU-level statistical transmission regulations (ESA 2010 / Regulation (EU) No 549/2013 and HICP regulations).
- The November 2024 ESS–ESCB MoU signals a shift toward broader microdata and confidential-data exchange between the ESS and ESCB following the 2024 revision of Regulation (EC) No 223/2009 and ongoing revision of Council Regulation (EC) No 2533/98.

### Gaps
- The text of specific bilateral "supplementary agreements" concluded under Section 1(4) of the 2021 Bundesbank–Destatis MoU (which govern exact operational cut-off times, pre-release embargo rules, and technical file schemas between Wiesbaden and Frankfurt) is not publicly posted on either institution's website.
- Whether the Bundesbank–Destatis MoU (which requires review "no later than after five years" from 13 September 2021, i.e., by September 2026) has been re-signed in late 2026 could not be verified from Destatis's public MoU page, which still reports "last revised in 2021."

## 2. What technical channels does Destatis provide, and what dedicated institutional channels exist for Bundesbank / ESCB / Eurostat exchange?

### Takeaway
A sharp technical boundary separates **public dissemination channels** from **institutional statistical pipelines**: Destatis serves the public via its GENESIS-Online REST/JSON API (which retired SOAP/XML and GET requests on 15 July 2025) alongside flat-file CSV and XLSX exports, whereas institutional ESS and ESCB data exchanges run over dedicated secure infrastructures—**eDAMIS** (Single Entry Point to Eurostat, >75% SDMX) and **ESCB-Net** (closed encrypted network between NCBs and the ECB using SDMX-EDI/GESMES and SDMX-ML).

### Cited Findings

#### A. Destatis Public Channels (GENESIS-Online & Open Data)
- **GENESIS-Online RESTful/JSON API:** Destatis's main public database, GENESIS-Online, is free under the *Datenlizenz Deutschland – Namensnennung – Version 2.0* license and exposes a RESTful/JSON web service (`https://www-genesis.destatis.de/genesisWS/rest/2020/`) for automated table, cube, catalogue, and search queries — [Destatis: GENESIS-Online API/Webservices und Weboberfläche](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Service/OpenData/genesis-api-webservice-oberflaeche.html); [Statistisches Bundesamt Official GitHub: GENESIS-Online](https://github.com/StatistischesBundesamt/GENESIS-Online).
- **15 July 2025 breaking protocol changes (SOAP retirement & POST-only):** According to Destatis's official GitHub documentation, **"From July 15, 2025 on the RESTful/JSON webservice interface of GENESIS-Online will no longer respond to GET requests. Also, SOAP/XML services will be retired entirely by this date."** — [Statistisches Bundesamt Official GitHub: GENESIS-Online](https://github.com/StatistischesBundesamt/GENESIS-Online).
- **Authentication & API tokens:** Registered users authenticate to the GENESIS-Online REST/JSON web service using either username/email and password or an individual **API token** generated inside the GENESIS-Online portal. Token authentication is restricted to **read-only** operations; managing batch jobs or user credentials still requires username/email and password — [Statistisches Bundesamt Official GitHub: GENESIS-Online](https://github.com/StatistischesBundesamt/GENESIS-Online).
- **Bulk web downloads, Flat File CSV, and *Statistische Berichte*:** Through the GENESIS web UI, users can export layout-oriented **XLSX** files or machine-readable **"Flat File CSV-Format"** ("tidy data" combining data and structured metadata). Destatis is also retiring legacy *Fachserien* PDF/table series and replacing them with *Statistische Berichte* containing linearized, machine-processable tables — [Destatis: GENESIS-Online API/Webservices und Weboberfläche](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Service/OpenData/genesis-api-webservice-oberflaeche.html).
- **Developer tooling (Python, VBA, R):** Destatis's official GitHub repository supplies both **Python** and **VBA** (Excel) example notebooks for table and cube downloads, and points R users to the community-maintained `restatis` package — [Statistisches Bundesamt Official GitHub: GENESIS-Online](https://github.com/StatistischesBundesamt/GENESIS-Online).
- **EVAS vs. SDMX in Destatis public exports:** GENESIS-Online tables and surveys are indexed by EVAS (*Einheitliches Verzeichnis der Statistiken*) codes and served via Destatis's proprietary REST/JSON schema rather than a native public SDMX REST endpoint; Destatis's SDMX formatting is primarily tied to ESS/Eurostat reporting — [Destatis: GENESIS-Online API/Webservices und Weboberfläche](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Service/OpenData/genesis-api-webservice-oberflaeche.html); [Eurostat CROS: Data exchange in the ESS](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/data-exchange-ess).

#### B. Institutional ESS & ESCB Channels (eDAMIS, SDMX, ESCB-Net, ECB Data Portal)
- **eDAMIS (ESS Single Entry Point to Eurostat):** National authorities across EU Member States and EFTA countries (including Destatis for ESA 2010 national accounts and HICP, and NCBs where applicable) transmit mandatory statistical datasets and metadata to Eurostat via **eDAMIS** (*electronic Data files Administration and Management Information System*), Eurostat's single entry point. As of November 2025, **more than 75%** of data transmitted to Eurostat follow the **SDMX** standard — [Eurostat CROS: Data exchange in the ESS (10 Nov 2025)](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/data-exchange-ess); [Eurostat CROS: EDAMIS short user guide (4 Mar 2026)](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/edamis-short-user-guide).
- **eDAMIS technical capabilities:** eDAMIS manages dataset definitions linked to **SDMX Data Structure Definitions (DSDs)** and **Metadata Structure Definitions (MSDs)** stored centrally in the **Euro SDMX Registry**, enforces reporting periodicities and legal deadlines, supports **encryption** for confidential datasets, integrates **SDMX Converter / SDMX RI** and **pre-validation services**, and returns automated validation feedback files to providers — [Eurostat CROS: EDAMIS short user guide (4 Mar 2026)](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/edamis-short-user-guide); [Eurostat CROS: Data exchange in the ESS (10 Nov 2025)](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/data-exchange-ess).
- **ESCB-Net (closed Eurosystem/ESCB network):** All confidential statistical information (CSI) exchanged between EU National Central Banks (such as Deutsche Bundesbank) and the ECB is encrypted and sender-authenticated; **"Most transmissions take place via the ESCB-Net (a closed network). In a few cases secure Internet is used (with encryption and authentication)."** In the ESCB, SDMX includes a mandatory confidentiality "flag" on CSI observations, and in the January–December 2024 reporting period zero confidentiality incidents were recorded — [ECB: Statistical Confidentiality Protection in the ESCB – Confidentiality Report 2024](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/pdf/statisticalconfidentialityprotectionreport2024.en.pdf).
- **SDMX-EDI (GESMES/TS) vs. SDMX-ML in the ESCB:** **SDMX-EDI (GESMES/TS)** has historically been the primary technical messaging standard used by the ECB to exchange statistical data and metadata with ESCB NCBs and international organizations, while **SDMX-ML** (XML, ISO/TS 17369) is used for web dissemination (including the **ECB Data Portal** and its **SDMX 2.1 RESTful web services**) and is **"now progressively used also in data and metadata exchange"** — [ECB: SDMX – statistical data exchange model](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_statistics/sdmx/html/index.en.html).
- **Clarification on "EXR":** In ECB statistical infrastructure, **EXR** is not a transmission network; it is the ECB's statistical dataflow code for **Exchange Rates** (daily reference rates since 1999) exposed via the ECB Data Portal's SDMX 2.1 RESTful API (`https://data-api.ecb.europa.eu/service/`) — [ECB: SDMX – statistical data exchange model](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_statistics/sdmx/html/index.en.html).
- **Bundesbank bilateral exchange with external organizations:** In Deutsche Bundesbank's Directorate General Statistics value chain, **Destatis, Eurostat, ECB, BIS, OECD, and IMF** appear explicitly on both the inbound side ("Data exchange, Downloads") and outbound side ("Data exchange") of the Central Statistics Infrastructure / SDMX Platform, distinct from primary reporter ingestion via Bundesbank's **ExtraNet** (file uploads and **AMS** online forms) — [Stephan Müller (Deutsche Bundesbank), "Creating Comprehensive Data Worlds using Standardization", BIS 9th Biennial IFC Conference, 31 Aug 2018, p. 6](https://www.bis.org/events/20180830/ninth-biennial-ifc-conference-are-post-crisis-statistical-initiatives-completed/mueller-0.pdf).

### Inferences
- Deutsche Bundesbank does **not** rely solely on scraping or polling Destatis's public GENESIS-Online web portal for core macro series; instead, national accounts, price statistics, and seasonal-adjustment inputs flow through bilateral institutional data exchange with Destatis and via shared European SDMX pipelines (eDAMIS / Eurostat / ESCB-Net), while GENESIS-Online's REST/JSON API serves public users and potentially ad hoc or non-core lookups.
- The coexistence of SDMX-EDI (GESMES/TS) and SDMX-ML inside the ESCB reflects long-standing legacy production reliability from Monetary Union preparations alongside progressive modernization toward SDMX-ML/JSON RESTful services.

### Gaps
- Exact technical transport protocol used today for the **bilateral Destatis ↔ Deutsche Bundesbank** direct file exchange (e.g., whether Destatis pushes files directly into Bundesbank's ExtraNet / SFTP / SDMX-ML exchange, or whether Bundesbank pulls German ESA 2010/HICP tables predominantly via Eurostat/ECB SDMX feeds after Destatis's eDAMIS upload) is not publicly specified in the 2021 MoU or public technical documentation.
- Public documentation does not specify the exact current adoption timeline of **SDMX 3.0** across eDAMIS and ESCB-Net production flows in 2025–2026.

## 3. How does Deutsche Bundesbank ingest Destatis and Eurostat macro series into its own systems?

### Takeaway
Deutsche Bundesbank ingests external institutional feeds (Destatis, Eurostat, ECB, BIS, OECD, IMF) and primary statistics into a multi-tier **Central Statistics Infrastructure** unified by an internal **SDMX Platform** spanning mainframe (`ZIS-DB`), relational (`SDW` on Oracle, `DOTSTAT` on MS SQL Server), and microdata (`IMIDIAS` / `House of Microdata` / `RDSC`) repositories. It co-developed **JDemetra+** with the National Bank of Belgium to execute X-13 seasonal and calendar adjustment on German macroeconomic and price indicators, and republishes both macro time series and real-time vintages publicly via its **SDMX Web Service REST API**.

### Cited Findings
- **Internal Central Statistics Infrastructure & databases:** According to Deutsche Bundesbank's Directorate General Statistics architecture presentation at the BIS IFC, statistical aggregates and macroeconomic indicators (spanning prices, national accounts, public finances, economic activity, real economy, balance of payments, financial markets, interest rates, yields, and securities) are stored across decentralized technical repositories unified by **SDMX (specialist and technical web-service interfaces, SDMX cubes, key families, and code lists)**:
  - **ZIS-DB** (mainframe Host, IMS HALDB, DB2) — storing statistical aggregates and macroeconomic indicators;
  - **DOTSTAT** (MS SQL Server);
  - **SDW** (Oracle DB);
  - **ZEUS** — administrative layer of the Central Statistics Infrastructure — [Stephan Müller (Deutsche Bundesbank), "Creating Comprehensive Data Worlds using Standardization", BIS 9th Biennial IFC Conference, 31 Aug 2018, pp. 6–7](https://www.bis.org/events/20180830/ninth-biennial-ifc-conference-are-post-crisis-statistical-initiatives-completed/mueller-0.pdf).
- **IMIDIAS, House of Microdata (HoM), and RDSC:** Mandated in 2013 to break down internal data silos across financial stability, research, monetary policy, and supervision, the Bundesbank built **IMIDIAS** (*Integrated Micro Data-based Information and Analysis System*), composed of three pillars: (1) the **House of Microdata (HoM)** (built directly on the SDMX data model and Central Statistics Infrastructure for internal Bundesbank analysts), (2) the **Research Data and Service Centre (RDSC / *Forschungsdaten- und Servicezentrum*)** providing regulated on-site/controlled access to anonymized microdata for external scientific researchers, and (3) an integrated data management layer using common identifiers and uniform SDMX code lists — [BIS IFC Report: "Data-sharing practices"](https://www.bis.org/publications/data-sharing-practices.pdf); [Stephan Müller (Deutsche Bundesbank), BIS 9th Biennial IFC Conference, 31 Aug 2018, p. 8](https://www.bis.org/events/20180830/ninth-biennial-ifc-conference-are-post-crisis-statistical-initiatives-completed/mueller-0.pdf).
- **Seasonal and calendar adjustment with JDemetra+ (co-developed by Bundesbank):** **JDemetra+**—officially recommended by the ESS and ESCB since 2015 as the reference software for seasonal and calendar adjustment—was co-developed by the **National Bank of Belgium** and **Deutsche Bundesbank**. Bundesbank explicitly documents that seasonal and calendar adjustment of German HICP data is carried out using **X-13 in JDemetra+ (version 2.2.2)** — [Deutsche Bundesbank: Harmonised Consumer Prices – Methodology and Quality](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/statistics/economic-activity-and-prices/harmonised-consumer-prices/methodology-and-quality-932144); [Destatis WISTA 4/2018: Linz, Fries & Völker, "Seasonal adjustment of short-term statistics using X-12-ARIMA and X13 in JDemetra+"](https://www.destatis.de/EN/Methods/WISTAScientificJournal/Downloads/seasonal-adjustment-042018.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2); [Deutsche Bundesbank: Advanced Topics in Seasonal Adjustment with JDemetra+](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/767818/4810043aa84e48cb8c81e11fa86ffa24/mL/advanced-topics-data.pdf).
- **Destatis transition to X13 in JDemetra+ (October 2026 milestone):** Destatis began transitioning from US Census Bureau X-12-ARIMA (v0.2.8) to X13 in JDemetra+ in late 2017 to comply with ESS Guidelines (where X13/TRAMO-SEATS are Alternative A and Germany's traditional *Berliner Verfahren* BV4.1 is Alternative C for international comparability). Destatis formally states that legacy **X-12-ARIMA and BV4.1 will be phased out entirely by October 2026** — [Destatis: Methods – Seasonal Adjustment](https://www.destatis.de/EN/Methods/Seasonal-adjustment/_node.html); [Destatis WISTA 4/2018: Linz, Fries & Völker](https://www.destatis.de/EN/Methods/WISTAScientificJournal/Downloads/seasonal-adjustment-042018.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2).
- **Bundesbank Time Series Database, Real-Time Database, and SDMX Web Service API:** Bundesbank republishes macroeconomic time series and real-time data vintages (including German output, national accounts, and HICP series) through its **Statistics Portal** (`statistiken.bundesbank.de`) and an open, unauthenticated HTTPS **SDMX Web Service REST API** (`https://api.statistiken.bundesbank.de/rest/`). The API supports **SDMX-JSON (default), SDMX-ML 2.1, and CSV** for data, and **SDMX-ML** for structural metadata (datastructures, dataflows, codelists, conceptschemes). Supported endpoints include single/wildcard/OR queries (`/data/{flowRef}/{key}`), full dataflow pulls (`/data/{flowRef}`), and batch POST queries (`/data/tsIdList`) — [Bundesbank Statistics: Bundesbank SDMX Web Service API](https://statistiken.bundesbank.de/statistiken-en/help-991452?article=bundesbank-sdmx-web-service-api-991448); [Deutsche Bundesbank: New real-time database for the German economy](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/press/press-releases/new-real-time-database-for-the-german-economy-670274).
- **FAME usage in the central banking peer group:** A BIS-IFC survey of 19 central banks and statistical agencies (*Future of Time Series*, presented October 2023, published in BIS IFC Bulletin 64, July 2026) found that **12 of 19** institutions currently use **FAME** (with 5 actively transitioning away), 2 previously used FAME and fully transitioned away, and 5 never used FAME and run bespoke systems—with no single replacement emerging (institutions migrating to SQL warehouses, NoSQL/HBase, or data lakehouses) — [Ryland Thomas (Bank of England), "Future of time series: preliminary results from a BIS-IFC survey of central banks and statistical agencies", BIS IFC Bulletin No. 64, July 2026](https://www.bis.org/2026-07/ifcb64_20.pdf).

### Inferences
- Because Bundesbank's 2018 Directorate General Statistics architecture explicitly documents `ZIS-DB` (IMS HALDB/DB2), `DOTSTAT` (MS SQL Server), and `SDW` (Oracle DB) behind an SDMX Platform—rather than naming FAME—its core official statistical production warehouse appears to rely on mainframe/relational SDMX-backed stores rather than FAME as its primary central repository.
- The harmonization of both Destatis and Deutsche Bundesbank onto **X-13 in JDemetra+** (with Destatis's October 2026 retirement of BV4.1 and X-12-ARIMA) removes a longstanding methodological divergence in German short-term and GDP seasonal adjustment.

### Gaps
- Individual central bank responses in the BIS-IFC *Future of Time Series* survey (2023/2026) are hosted on restricted eBIS spreadsheets and not disaggregated by institution publicly; therefore, it cannot be confirmed from public sources whether specific Deutsche Bundesbank economics/forecasting divisions still run FAME locally alongside the Statistics Department's ZIS-DB/SDW/DOTSTAT infrastructure.
- Whether Deutsche Bundesbank has modernized or retired its mainframe `ZIS-DB` (IMS HALDB/DB2) layer between 2018 and 2026 (e.g., migrating toward a cloud/lakehouse or upgraded SDMX 3.0 `.Stat` stack) is not documented in publicly available technical architecture papers.

## 4. Does Deutsche Bundesbank also use commercial aggregators or manual Excel downloads for certain supplementary/international series?

### Takeaway
Yes. Official Deutsche Bundesbank architecture documentation explicitly confirms direct ingestion of commercial market-data feeds (such as Markit and Thomson Reuters) into Directorate General Statistics, while Bundesbank publications and market operations regularly use Bloomberg, Thomson Reuters Datastream, and Haver Analytics for financial market and international macroeconomic series. On the user-consumption side, Excel remains formally embedded via an internal **Excel connector** tied to the Bundesbank's Central Statistics Infrastructure, and both Bundesbank and Destatis continue to support XLS/CSV/VBA workflows.

### Cited Findings
- **Commercial feeds in Directorate General Statistics:** Deutsche Bundesbank's official Directorate General Statistics value-chain diagram explicitly includes **"Commercial data sources: MarkIT, Thomson-Reuters, ..."** as a primary inbound data stream feeding into its statistical databases alongside official international/national agencies (ECB, BIS, OECD, IMF, Eurostat, Destatis) — [Stephan Müller (Deutsche Bundesbank), "Creating Comprehensive Data Worlds using Standardization", BIS 9th Biennial IFC Conference, 31 Aug 2018, p. 6](https://www.bis.org/events/20180830/ninth-biennial-ifc-conference-are-post-crisis-statistical-initiatives-completed/mueller-0.pdf).
- **Bloomberg, Datastream, and Haver in Bundesbank reports and operations:** Deutsche Bundesbank publications (such as the *Financial Stability Review* and *Monthly Reports*) cite commercial data providers including Bloomberg, Thomson Reuters Datastream, and Haver Analytics for international macro and financial-market indicators, and Bundesbank uses Bloomberg auction infrastructure (e.g., AUPD function) for open-market operations such as PSPP reverse auctions — [Deutsche Bundesbank: Financial Stability Review 2013](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/654260/fe96f1e8092d9438ae5e190c23e20b8e/mL/2013-finanzstabilitaetsbericht-data.pdf); [Deutsche Bundesbank: PSPP Auctions](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/tasks/monetary-policy/open-market-operations/pspp-auctions/pspp-auctions-626008).
- **Commercial aggregators redistributing Bundesbank data (distinct from internal use):** Commercial platforms such as **Macrobond** and **Haver Analytics** ingest and redistribute Deutsche Bundesbank macroeconomic series to external economists and financial-sector subscribers (often blended with Bloomberg via connectors) — [Macrobond: Deutsche Bundesbank data coverage](https://www.macrobond.com); [Haver Analytics](https://www.haver.com).
- **Internal Excel connector in Bundesbank's statistical stack:** Rather than relying solely on ad hoc manual copy-paste spreadsheets, Deutsche Bundesbank's Central Statistics Infrastructure explicitly provides an **"Excel connector"** alongside BI tools and time-series transformation utilities for internal self-service evaluation, analysis, and publication table/chart preparation — [Stephan Müller (Deutsche Bundesbank), BIS 9th Biennial IFC Conference, 31 Aug 2018, p. 6](https://www.bis.org/events/20180830/ninth-biennial-ifc-conference-are-post-crisis-statistical-initiatives-completed/mueller-0.pdf).
- **Continued public/analyst XLS and VBA support:** Bundesbank's *Seasonally adjusted business statistics* portal continues to offer downloadable ZIP bundles of PDF, **XLS**, and CSV files (capped at 2,000 time series per selection), and Destatis's official GENESIS-Online GitHub repository maintains **VBA** (Excel) code examples alongside Python for programmatic table downloads — [Deutsche Bundesbank: Seasonally adjusted business statistics](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/statistics/economic-activity-and-prices/-/seasonally-adjusted-business-statistics-808774); [Statistisches Bundesamt Official GitHub: GENESIS-Online](https://github.com/StatistischesBundesamt/GENESIS-Online).
- **Broader central-bank shift away from Excel toward R/Python:** Across the 19 central banks and statistical agencies surveyed by the BIS-IFC, respondents reported that **"Excel use is falling, with analysts increasingly using open source analytical tools such as R and Python, and specialist data visualisation tools such as Tableau,"** which is a major driver behind moving away from Excel-centric legacy workflows like FAME's Excel populator add-in — [Ryland Thomas (Bank of England), BIS IFC Bulletin No. 64, July 2026](https://www.bis.org/2026-07/ifcb64_20.pdf).

### Inferences
- Commercial aggregators (Bloomberg, LSEG/Thomson Reuters Datastream, Markit/S&P Global, Haver Analytics) at Deutsche Bundesbank serve primarily to supply **financial market prices, corporate/credit benchmarks, and non-EU/global macroeconomic series**, whereas official German GDP, CPI/HICP, and euro area macro series come through official Destatis, Eurostat, and ECB channels.
- Excel inside Deutsche Bundesbank has shifted from manual file downloads toward database-connected self-service add-ins (Excel connectors wired to the SDMX/Central Statistics Infrastructure), while econometric and data-science workflows increasingly rely on R and Python (including `JDemetra+` / `rjdverse` tooling).

## 5. What has Bundesbank or the ECB published in BIS IFC papers, Bundesbank Monthly Reports, or Eurostat/SDMX papers about automating NSO-to-central-bank ingestion, eliminating manual spreadsheets, and SDMX standardization?

### Takeaway
Deutsche Bundesbank, the ECB, and the BIS Irving Fisher Committee (IFC) have published extensively on moving from siloed, manual statistical production toward **metadata-driven SDMX standardization**, **"single source of truth"** microdata architectures (`IMIDIAS` / `House of Microdata`), and cross-institutional data sharing (`INEXDA`, ESS–ESCB MoU). These publications stress that IT automation alone fails without semantic harmonization (shared code lists and DSDs) and domain expertise, and document a sector-wide transition away from Excel/FAME silos toward open-source R/Python and standardized API/SDMX pipelines.

### Cited Findings
- **Three-step SDMX data integration model (Bundesbank, BIS IFC 2018):** In his 2018 BIS 9th Biennial IFC Conference presentation, Stephan Müller (Deutsche Bundesbank) frames central bank data integration as a three-step progression: (1) **Logical Centralization** (common storage, authorization, and access), (2) **Uniform Data Modeling Method / Order System** (enabling rule-based, automatable treatment), and (3) **Semantic Harmonization** (identical concepts, methods, and code lists enabling actual content linking). He warns against "Collectomania" alongside yawning data gaps, emphasizes that **SDMX works seamlessly for both macro aggregates and granular microdata** (via multidimensional cubes and uniform code lists in the House of Microdata), and cautions that "Automation or lack of expertise could lead to comparing apples and oranges" — [Stephan Müller (Deutsche Bundesbank), "Creating Comprehensive Data Worlds using Standardization", BIS 9th Biennial IFC Conference, 31 Aug 2018](https://www.bis.org/events/20180830/ninth-biennial-ifc-conference-are-post-crisis-statistical-initiatives-completed/mueller-0.pdf).
- **Remaining standardization gaps identified by Bundesbank:** Müller (2018) explicitly highlights unresolved structural challenges: the absence of globally consistent code lists, tension between open-source/global sharing and statutory confidentiality constraints, silo thinking across divisions, and underinvestment in data literacy — [Stephan Müller (Deutsche Bundesbank), BIS 9th Biennial IFC Conference, 31 Aug 2018, pp. 5, 9](https://www.bis.org/events/20180830/ninth-biennial-ifc-conference-are-post-crisis-statistical-initiatives-completed/mueller-0.pdf).
- **BIS IFC Data-Sharing Practices report (IMIDIAS, RDSC, INEXDA, FBDC):** The BIS IFC report highlights Deutsche Bundesbank's **IMIDIAS** initiative as an international benchmark for overcoming internal "data silos" and heterogeneous quality-assurance practices via standardized metadata and common identifiers. It also highlights Germany's cross-institutional RDSC coordination under the **German Data Forum** (linking Bundesbank's RDSC with Destatis/NSI RDSCs), the **INEXDA** network (13 central banks + NSIs launched in 2017 to harmonize granular metadata schemas), and Germany's **Financial Big Data Cluster (FBDC)** — [BIS IFC Report: "Data-sharing practices"](https://www.bis.org/publications/data-sharing-practices.pdf).
- **BIS IFC Bulletin 64 (July 2026) — "Future of Time Series" survey:** Published in BIS IFC Bulletin 64 (from the October 2023 IFC–Bank of Italy workshop), Ryland Thomas summarizes central bank and NSO lessons on modernizing end-to-end time-series ingestion ("getting data 'in the building' to producing briefing/publications"). Key conclusions include aiming for a **"single source of truth"** ("Data is collected once and used multiple times" / "produce micro-level data and then aggregate at one central institution"), decoupling time-series storage from analytics so analysts can use **R, Python, and Tableau** instead of FAME 4GL and Excel, and solving the hardest remaining bottleneck: managing heterogeneous incoming data types and metadata searchability — [Ryland Thomas (Bank of England), "Future of time series: preliminary results from a BIS-IFC survey of central banks and statistical agencies", BIS IFC Bulletin No. 64, July 2026](https://www.bis.org/2026-07/ifcb64_20.pdf).
- **Eurostat/ESS & ECB SDMX institutional publications:** Eurostat CROS documentation confirms that SDMX adoption has surpassed **75%** of all ESS transmissions to Eurostat via eDAMIS, supported by common open-source/shared tools (Euro SDMX Registry, SDMX Converter, SDMX RI, pre-validation services). The ECB's SDMX documentation and 2024 Statistical Confidentiality Protection Report document how SDMX (both SDMX-EDI/GESMES and SDMX-ML, plus SDMX 2.1 RESTful APIs on the ECB Data Portal) embeds mandatory confidentiality flags directly into automated ESCB-Net data pipelines — [Eurostat CROS: Data exchange in the ESS (10 Nov 2025)](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/data-exchange-ess); [ECB: SDMX – statistical data exchange model](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_statistics/sdmx/html/index.en.html); [ECB: Statistical Confidentiality Protection in the ESCB – Confidentiality Report 2024](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/pdf/statisticalconfidentialityprotectionreport2024.en.pdf).
- **Joint Destatis–Bundesbank methodological publications on seasonal adjustment:** Destatis's scientific journal *WISTA* (4/2018) documents the operational alignment of German short-term economic and GDP seasonal adjustment with ESS/ESCB guidelines through the open-source JDemetra+ platform co-developed with Bundesbank, reducing duplicative legacy toolchains (X-12-ARIMA and BV4.1) — [Destatis WISTA 4/2018: Linz, Fries & Völker, "Seasonal adjustment of short-term statistics using X-12-ARIMA and X13 in JDemetra+"](https://www.destatis.de/EN/Methods/WISTAScientificJournal/Downloads/seasonal-adjustment-042018.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2).

### Inferences
- Across Bundesbank, ECB, and BIS IFC literature, "eliminating manual spreadsheets" is framed less as banning Excel outright and more as **replacing manual file-handling silos with metadata-driven SDMX pipelines and single-source-of-truth warehouses**, onto which analysts connect via APIs, R/Python packages, BI dashboards, or database-linked Excel connectors.
- The European statistical model (ESS + ESCB) achieves unusually high automation between NSOs and central banks because legal mandates (Reg. 223/2009, Reg. 2533/98, ESA 2010) are paired with jointly governed technical registries (Euro SDMX Registry), transmission hubs (eDAMIS, ESCB-Net), and shared open-source statistical software (JDemetra+).

### Gaps
- While Bundesbank staff have published prominently on microdata integration (IMIDIAS/House of Microdata/RDSC) and SDMX DSD design for microdata, we found no recent (2024–2026) standalone Deutsche Bundesbank Monthly Report article or BIS IFC paper dedicated specifically to the internal ETL pipeline that ingests Destatis GDP/CPI releases into Bundesbank's macroeconomic forecasting models.
- Detailed post-2023 migration case studies showing how Deutsche Bundesbank specifically handles **SDMX 3.0** or the ECB's **IReF (Integrated Reporting Framework)** / **BIRD** transition for macro-to-micro ingestion were outside the retrieved primary documents and remain a gap.
