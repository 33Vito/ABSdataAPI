# Central banks stitch hybrid statistical pipelines

> **Related:** this report provides the cross-jurisdiction [design rationale](#design-rationale-for-the-absdataapi-phase-1-pipeline) for the ABSdataAPI pipeline. The implementation evidence
> (live-tested endpoints, verified dataflows and the Phase 1 architecture) is in
> [ABS API research notes](../abs-api-research.md), [§9 PoC architecture](../abs-api-research.md#9-suggested-poc-architecture) in particular.
> The source notes for each jurisdiction are indexed under [Research notes](#research-notes).

Central banks in the four primary OECD jurisdictions examined—Australia, the United States, Germany/Eurosystem, and South Korea—plus the United Kingdom as a pre-release governance benchmark do not rely on a single public API or manual spreadsheet to ingest national macroeconomic statistics such as GDP and CPI; instead, the documented evidence shows **hybrid, bifurcated setups** combining domestic official channels with commercial aggregators (**Haver Analytics, LSEG/Refinitiv, Bloomberg, CEIC, Macrobond**) for international series and market prices. In the Bank for International Settlements Irving Fisher Committee (BIS IFC) survey covering 52 central bank jurisdictions overall, among SDMX-using respondents rating perceived relevance (Graph 4), **81%** rated the Statistical Data and Metadata eXchange (**SDMX**) standard as extremely or very relevant for outbound reporting, compared with **38%** for inbound collection—highlighting a perceived asymmetry between outbound reporting and inbound collection rather than measuring specific transport-protocol shares across all 52 institutions ([BIS IFC Report No. 17, Feb. 2025](https://www.bis.org/publications/ifc-report-17-sdmx-adoption-and-use-open-source-tools.pdf)).

Crucially, primary-source scrutiny across these jurisdictions reveals a sharp evidentiary divide: while National Statistical Offices (NSOs) publicly document their REST/SDMX APIs and Excel portals in detail, **the central banks studied rarely publish the exact wire, file, or API transport protocols used inside their firewalls to ingest domestic GDP and CPI releases**. Where primary documentation confirms an institutional data source or pre-release window without specifying the underlying wire protocol, the comparison tables in this report explicitly record **`Transport: Unknown (not publicly documented)`** rather than inferring that a central bank consumes its NSO's public API or spreadsheets.

## Outbound reporting runs on SDMX while inbound ingestion stays fragmented

*Source notes: [global-standards-and-methods.md](notes/global-standards-and-methods.md)*

Global central bank data architectures are shaped by a structural asymmetry: in most jurisdictions, central banks compile monetary, financial, and balance-of-payments statistics while acting as **consumers** of real-sector macroeconomic series (GDP, CPI, and labour force data) produced by NSOs or line ministries ([BIS IFC Report No. 17, Feb. 2025](https://www.bis.org/publications/ifc-report-17-sdmx-adoption-and-use-open-source-tools.pdf)). Although **79%** of surveyed central banks now use or implement ISO 17369 **SDMX**—up from **64%** in 2015, with another **14%** planning adoption—most treat SDMX as a "last-mile export" format driven by IMF SDDS Plus National Summary Data Pages (NSDPs), BIS, and ECB reporting mandates ([BIS IFC Report No. 17, Feb. 2025](https://www.bis.org/publications/ifc-report-17-sdmx-adoption-and-use-open-source-tools.pdf); [IMF DSBB SDDS Plus Overview](https://dsbb.imf.org/sdds-plus/overview)).

Version lag compounds this friction: **59%** of SDMX version responses remain on **SDMX 2.1** (2011) and **35%** on **SDMX 2.0**, with just **6%** on **SDMX 3.0** (as individual central banks often run multiple versions concurrently) even as **SDMX 3.1** shipped in May 2025 ([BIS IFC Report No. 17, Feb. 2025](https://www.bis.org/publications/ifc-report-17-sdmx-adoption-and-use-open-source-tools.pdf); [SDMX Technical Specifications](https://sdmx.org/?page_id=5008)). Parallel domain standards (**52%** using XBRL for prudential reporting, alongside ISO 20022 and DDI) force institutions to maintain multi-protocol pipelines; for instance, the Central Bank of Malta reported in 2025 that its outbound ECB (EXDI) and IMF SDDS Plus transmissions use SDMX, whereas domestic inter-agency exchange still runs over **non-SDMX SFTP** alongside static Excel dissemination ([Central Bank of Malta, SDMX 2025](https://www.sdmx2025.org/plenary/SESSION_4/Session%204_P2%20-%20SDMX%20Oriented%20Dissemination%20System%20Implementation%20Case%20Study%20at%20the%20Central%20Bank%20of%20Malta%20%28I.%20Cagan%29.pdf)).

Inside central bank walls, legacy time-series infrastructure is undergoing a slow, uncoordinated transition. In an anonymized 2023 BIS IFC survey of 19 central banks and statistical agencies, **14 of 19 (~74%)** had relied on **FIS FAME** historically or currently: **12 still use FAME** (with **5 actively transitioning away**), **2 have fully exited**, **5 run bespoke systems**, and **zero non-users plan to adopt FAME** ([Ryland Thomas / BoE, BIS IFC Bulletin No. 64, July 2026](https://www.bis.org/2026-07/ifcb64_20.pdf); [BIS IFC Guidance Note No. 4, Dec. 2023](https://www.bis.org/publications/central-banks-use-time-series-products.pdf)). Because those survey responses are anonymized, individual respondents cannot be identified from the counts; however, separately documented institutional initiatives show the same trajectory—notably the Bank of England's **Future FAME** programme (active under Audit and Risk Committee oversight as of February 2026) and the ECB's post-2019 project to replace its legacy macro statistics platform with an **Apache Hadoop** stack ([BoE Court Minutes, 12 Feb. 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/minutes/2026/may/court-minutes-12-february-2026); [BIS IFC Guidance Note No. 4, Dec. 2023](https://www.bis.org/publications/central-banks-use-time-series-products.pdf)).

Surveyed central banks retain FAME for its native dynamic formulas and tight **Excel Populator** add-in, yet are leaving because **Excel usage is declining** in favor of **Python, R, and Tableau**, while FAME struggles with microdata, metadata searchability, and dwindling specialist talent ([Ryland Thomas / BoE, BIS IFC Bulletin No. 64, July 2026](https://www.bis.org/2026-07/ifcb64_20.pdf)). Because no single commercial replacement has emerged, institutions are dispersing across SQL data warehouses, NoSQL/HBase, open-source **`.Stat Suite`** stacks, and metadata-driven lakehouses such as the BIS's **MEDAL** platform built on SDMX 3.0, Fusion Metadata Registry (FMR), and **`pysdmx`**—where open-source tooling now accounts for **51%** of central bank SDMX installations with **83%** satisfaction ([BIS IFC Report No. 17, Feb. 2025](https://www.bis.org/publications/ifc-report-17-sdmx-adoption-and-use-open-source-tools.pdf); [OECD / SIS-CC `.Stat Suite`](https://siscc.org/stat-suite/)).

Even where public NSO APIs are available without charge, central banks continue to procure commercial macroeconomic aggregators (**Haver Analytics, Macrobond, Bloomberg, CEIC, LSEG/Refinitiv**), evidenced by ECB data tenders ([EU TED 174270-2019](https://ted.europa.eu/en/notice/-/detail/174270-2019)), U.S. federal procurement notices ([SAM.gov Notice](https://sam.gov/opp/fd90de6ad08e4b8e8357d99926724931/view)), and central bank publication attributions. Taken together, BIS IFC findings on standard fragmentation and central bank reliance on econometric toolchains (`MATLAB, STATA, EViews`), alongside platform documentation such as Macrobond's, point to five practical burdens commercial platforms address for cross-country monitoring and research: normalizing incompatible foreign NSO schemas, preserving **point-in-time (PIT) revision vintages**, insulating workflows from breaking API endpoints under vendor support, maintaining break-in-series metadata, and supplying turn-key connectors for Excel, MATLAB, EViews, R, and Python ([BIS IFC Guidance Note No. 4, Dec. 2023](https://www.bis.org/publications/central-banks-use-time-series-products.pdf); [Macrobond Documentation](https://help.macrobond.com/application-overview/mb-introduction/)).

## Embargo rules split public APIs from ring-fenced bilateral delivery

In Australia, the United States, and the United Kingdom, strict equality-of-access regimes distinguish simultaneous public release from tightly restricted pre-release windows. Under IMF SDDS/SDDS Plus Integrity standards, authorities commit to Advance Release Calendars, simultaneous and equal public access, and public disclosure of any internal government pre-release access ([IMF DSBB SDDS Plus Overview](https://dsbb.imf.org/sdds-plus/overview)). These IMF standards govern access equality and disclosure rather than mandating a particular non-API transport mechanism, though in the cases documented below, exceptional pre-release arrangements operate separately from public release portals.

### Australia pairs an embargo-lift API with opaque RBA plumbing

*Source notes: [australia-rba.md](notes/australia-rba.md). Implementation evidence: [ABS API research notes](../abs-api-research.md)*

The Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) compiles Australia's GDP (*Australian National Accounts*, Cat. No. 5206.0), Labour Force (Cat. No. 6202.0), and Consumer Price Index (Cat. No. 6401.0)—having transitioned to a **complete monthly CPI** by late 2025/early 2026—with simultaneous publication at **11:30 am Canberra time** ([ABS Pre-Release Policy, 20 Oct. 2026](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics); [RBA Changes to Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/changes-to-tables.html)). On the public dissemination side, the ABS operates a two-tier API alongside traditional downloadable Excel time-series spreadsheets and data cubes on `abs.gov.au`, a dual-channel setup mirrored by the open-source R package `{readabs}` ([readabs GitHub README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MattCowgill/readabs/master/README.md)).

Launched live on **20 February 2025**, the key-gated production **ABS Indicator API** (`indicator.api.abs.gov.au`, SDMX XML/JSON/CSV) **publishes as soon as the 11:30 am embargo lifts**—an operational release timing stated in ABS documentation rather than a contractual SLA—but is restricted to **22 curated headline dataflows** returning whole datasets without custom dimensional filters ([ABS Indicator API](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/application-programming-interfaces-apis/indicator-api); [ABS Pre-Release Policy, 20 Oct. 2026](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics); [ABS API research notes](../abs-api-research.md)). By contrast, the unauthenticated **ABS Data API** (`data.api.abs.gov.au/rest/`, SDMX 2.1, ~1,200+ dataflows including active `CPI` 2.0.0 and reinstated `CPI_Q` 1.0.0 alongside ceased `CPI_M` 1.2.0) and **ABS Data Explorer** (which replaced retired ABS.Stat) remain designated **Beta**, explicitly warning that availability is not guaranteed and post-embargo updates may lag `abs.gov.au`; live testing documented in [ABS API research notes](../abs-api-research.md) also confirms documented **30-second timeout and 10 MB response-size gateway limits** as well as an unreliable **`updatedAfter`** parameter ([ABS Data API User Guide](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/application-programming-interfaces-apis/data-api-user-guide); [ABS Data Explorer User Guide](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/application-programming-interfaces-apis/data-explorer-user-guide)).

Internally, the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) has publicly modernized its technology backbone through its **Core Modernisation Program (CoreMod)**, restructured its Enterprise Data Office into the **Information Department**, and issued a **February 2025 RFP** confirming a centralized **Enterprise Data Platform (EDP)** comprising **IBM InfoSphere Information Server**, **Cloudera Data Platform/ML**, **Microsoft Azure SQL Managed Instance**, **Posit** (R/Python), **Tableau**, and **Power BI** ([RBA Annual Report 2024](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/annual-reports/rba/2024/); [RBA Annual Report 2025](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/annual-reports/rba/2025/); [techpartner.news, 11 Feb. 2025](https://www.techpartner.news/news/rba-seeks-managed-service-provider-for-enterprise-data-platform-614931)). RBA *Chart Pack Data Availability* documentation draws a crisp source split: international macro and market series rely heavily on commercial vendors (**LSEG/Refinitiv**, **CEIC Data**, **Markit Economics**, **Bloomberg**, and housing providers **Cotality/CoreLogic, APM, and Residex**), whereas every domestic GDP, CPI, and household income series cites **ABS Catalogue Numbers** directly, complemented by joint ABS–RBA microdata access (**BLADE** and **WHAM**) ([RBA Chart Pack Data Availability](https://www.rba.gov.au/chart-pack/data-availability.html); [ABS and RBA Joint Conferences](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/other-confs/abs-and-rba-joint-conferences/)).

Outbound, however, direct inspection of the RBA's Statistical Tables portal (`rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/`) in November 2026 shows **234 CSV links**, **97 Excel links**, and **zero public API or SDMX endpoints** on that portal ([RBA Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/)). Tables such as **G1** (`g1-data.csv`, spliced back to June 1922, combining joint ABS/RBA headline and underlying series—including trimmed mean and weighted median carrying `Source: ABS / RBA`—with RBA-computed tradables, non-tradables, and excluding-volatile decompositions carrying `Source: RBA`), **H1** (`h1-data.csv`, quarterly GDP back to September 1959 carrying `Source: ABS`), and the new monthly CPI **Table G4** (launched 29 January 2026) prove the RBA runs an internal mapping and transformation layer rather than mirroring raw ABS files ([RBA Table G1 CSV](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/g1-data.csv); [RBA Table H1 CSV](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/h1-data.csv); [RBA Changes to Statistical Tables](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/changes-to-tables.html)).

Pre-release governance further constrains automated early ingestion. The ABS *Policy on Pre-Release Access* (updated 20 October 2026) confirms ABS-hosted physical secure **"lock-ups"** for nine market-sensitive releases—including monthly CPI and quarterly GDP—where attendees face strict no-communication rules until 11:30 am, and grants **day-before GDP access exclusively to approved Commonwealth Treasury officials**; notably, **the policy does not name the RBA** for either lock-ups or day-before access ([ABS Pre-Release Policy, 20 Oct. 2026](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/policy-pre-release-access-abs-statistics)). Critically, the primary-source record does not publicly document the RBA's exact internal transport pipeline for ABS GDP and CPI releases (Indicator API vs. Data API vs. automated Excel parsing vs. private transfer), and public AusTender and RBA records likewise do not confirm whether the RBA licenses FAME, Haver, or Macrobond.

### United States decentralizes agencies while FRED and Haver harmonize feeds

*Source notes: [united-states-fed.md](notes/united-states-fed.md)*

Unlike centralized NSO models, U.S. macroeconomic production is decentralized across **13 Principal Statistical Agencies** coordinated by the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), splitting core monetary-policy data across three cabinet-level bureaus: the **Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA)** for GDP and PCE, the **Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)** for CPI and payrolls, and the **U.S. Census Bureau** for retail, housing, and durable goods ([CRS IN12197](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12197.html)). Each agency runs a distinct public technical channel: BEA exposes a single-endpoint HTTPS REST API (`apps.bea.gov/api/data`, JSON/XML, free 36-character UserID, capped at **100 requests/min**, **100 MB/min**, and **30 errors/min**) plus web CSV/Excel tables ([BEA API User Guide, Apr. 2026](https://apps.bea.gov/api/_pdf/bea_web_service_api_user_guide.pdf)); BLS operates REST **API v1** (25 queries/day) and **v2** (500 queries/day, 50 series/query, 20 years/query, burst limit 50 req/10s) alongside bulk tab-delimited flat files (`download.bls.gov/pub/time.series/`) paired with strict HTTP 403 anti-bot enforcement ([BLS API Features](https://www.bls.gov/bls/api_features.htm); [BLS API FAQs](https://www.bls.gov/developers/api_faqs.htm)); and Census provides its key-gated **Economic Indicators Time Series (EITS)** REST API and bulk downloads ([Census EITS API](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/economic-indicators.html)).

Pre-release rules reinforce simultaneous public consumption. **OMB Statistical Policy Directive No. 3** (1985, amended effective **19 February 2024** solely to shorten the Executive Branch public-comment wait from one hour to **30 minutes**) grants standing pre-release access **to the President through the Chair of the Council of Economic Advisers (CEA)**, caps any other logged embargoed access (such as Commerce Department policy officials) at **30 minutes**, and prohibits public data banks from receiving data prior to official release ([OMB Directive No. 3, 50 FR 38932](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2018-05/federalregister09251985.pdf); [OMB Update, 89 FR 11873, Feb. 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/02/15/2024-02972/update-of-statistical-policy-directive-no-3-compilation-release-and-evaluation-of-principal-federal); [BEA Security and Release Procedures, Dec. 2025](https://www.bea.gov/system/files/2025-12/Security-and-Release-Procedures.pdf)). Neither Directive No. 3 nor BEA's December 2025 procedures codifies standing pre-release access for the Federal Reserve, and the Department of Labor permanently closed physical media lock-ups on **3 June 2020** (`85 FR 31810`) ([DOL Lock-Up Discontinuation, May 2020](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/05/27/2020-11297/announcing-discontinuation-of-the-dol-lock-up-facility-for-participating-news-media-organizations)).

To harmonize this fragmented landscape, the Federal Reserve System relies on two distinct documented layers. At the **Board of Governors**, Cannon and Krug (2006) documented two internal **FIS FAME** production databases housing **~62,000 time series** from **more than 60 sources** and **~100 releases**, governed by custom Townsend Greenspan-derived nomenclature, 15 FAME metadata attributes, an SQL Data Release Calendar, a restricted-wiki Data Documentation System, and a real-time snapshot store—later complemented by Board-authored `fame` R tooling and a **Python** implementation of the flagship **FRB/US** model in April 2022 ([Cannon & Krug, IASSIST Quarterly 2006](https://iassistquarterly.com/public/pdfs/iqvol301cannon.pdf); [CRAN `fame`](https://rdrr.io/cran/fame/); [Federal Reserve Board, FRB/US](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-about.htm)). Across the Reserve Banks and federal regulators, **Haver Analytics** (`USECON` and related standardized databases) is prominently cited alongside primary BEA/BLS feeds—for example, in the Cleveland Fed's daily **Inflation Nowcasting**—while recurring U.S. federal procurement notices appear on SAM.gov across executive and regulatory agencies (note that Board procurement largely sits outside standard FAR reporting) ([Haver Analytics](https://www.haver.com/our-data); [Cleveland Fed Inflation Nowcasting](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/inflation-nowcasting); [SAM.gov Notice](https://sam.gov/opp/fd90de6ad08e4b8e8357d99926724931/view)). Outbound, the Board—which pioneered central bank SDMX-ML dissemination via its **Data Download Program (DDP)** in April 2006—is retiring DDP between **December 2025 and 1 July 2027**, consolidating public API and bulk delivery into the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis's **FRED** platform ([Fed Press Release, Apr. 2006](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20060419a.htm); [Fed DDP and FRED Partnership, Nov. 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/data-download-fred-information.htm)).

For **FRED and ALFRED** (ArchivaL Federal Reserve Economic Data), primary St. Louis Fed sources confirm the high-level ingestion workflow without disclosing per-agency wire protocols. A St. Louis Fed *On the Economy* engineer interview (*"Bringing the Data to FRED"*) documents that FRED collects data from source websites and feeds it through an automated load pipeline ([St. Louis Fed *On the Economy*, Mar. 2021](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2021/march/bringing-data-to-fred)), while FRED Help (*"How Does FRED Update Its Database"*) and official FRED/ALFRED documentation confirm electronic download, validation, and bi-temporal archival (`observation_date`, `realtime_start`, `realtime_end`) preserving every BEA GDP and BLS revision vintage, supplemented by human Data Desk intervention for structural breaks and panels such as FRED-MD ([FRED/ALFRED Overview](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/help/about/about-fred/what-is-fred); [St. Louis Fed, *Have You Met FRED?*](https://www.stlouisfed.org/timely-topics/have-you-met-fred); [McCracken & Ng, St. Louis Fed WP 2015-012](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/frbsl_wp/2015-012.pdf)). However, neither the specific per-agency transport protocols into FRED (whether BEA NIPA or BLS CPI ingestion hits REST APIs, FTP/bulk flat files, or custom parsers) nor the Board of Governors' current 2025–2026 internal ingestion split (Haver Analytics vs. direct BEA/BLS feeds, and current FAME vs. cloud/SQL deployment) is publicly documented in primary sources.

### United Kingdom logs 60-minute exceptional MPC drops without exposing transport

The United Kingdom provides the clearest global benchmark of how strict embargo governance reshapes NSO-to-central-bank delivery. Effective **1 July 2017**, the Office for National Statistics (ONS) ended routine pre-release access for all government and external users except under **exceptional circumstances** approved by the National Statistician and published in a public log ([ONS Pre-release Access Policy & Public Log, updated 22 Oct. 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/aboutus/transparencyandgovernance/prereleaseaccess)). Because Bank of England Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meetings periodically collide with release mornings, the ONS public log documents recurring exceptional authorizations for **CPI, Labour Market Statistics, and PAYE Real Time Information** across 2025–2026. Specifically, the **11 September 2026 ONS–Bank of England exchange of letters** between BoE Deputy Governor Clare Lombardelli and ONS's James Benford documents direct, pushed exceptional pre-release delivery of requested releases **exactly 60 minutes prior to the start of the MPC Indicative Meeting** on Monday 14 September 2026 ([ONS–BoE Exchange of Letters, 11 Sept. 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/news/statementsandletters/exchangeoflettersbetweenthebankofenglandandtheofficefornationalstatisticsforexceptionalprereleaseaccesstoonspublicationsseptember2026)). Even in this unusually transparent disclosure, however, the published letters do not specify the underlying delivery mechanism (such as encrypted email, SFTP, or a closed portal): **`Transport: Unknown (not publicly documented)`**.

## Institutional networks and hybrid compilation reshape European and Korean pipelines

Where Anglo-American central banks sit largely outside formal statistical transmission networks for domestic real-sector series, Germany (within the Eurosystem) and South Korea represent two alternative architectures: **supranational SDMX network integration** in Europe, and **central bank GDP production** fed by statutory administrative microdata in South Korea.

### Germany routes European statistics through eDAMIS and ESCB-Net

*Source notes: [germany-bundesbank.md](notes/germany-bundesbank.md)*

Germany divides official statistical production between **Destatis** (*Statistisches Bundesamt*, BStatG § 3)—the sole producer of German GDP (non-financial national accounts), national CPI, and German HICP—and **Deutsche Bundesbank** (BBankG § 18), which compiles quarterly financial accounts and balance of payments and serves as Destatis's seasonal-adjustment partner ([Destatis National Accounts](https://www.destatis.de/EN/Themes/Economy/National-Accounts-Domestic-Product/_node.html); [Bundesbank Financial Accounts](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/statistics/macroeconomic-accounting-systems/financial-accounts/financial-accounts-792676); [Bundesbank HICP](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/statistics/economic-activity-and-prices/harmonised-consumer-prices/harmonised-index-of-consumer-prices-932146)). Legally, their exchange bridges Europe's two statistical pillars—the **European Statistical System (ESS)** under Regulation (EC) No 223/2009 and the **European System of Central Banks (ESCB)** under Council Regulation (EC) No 2533/98—operationalized by the bilateral **Bundesbank–Destatis Memorandum of Understanding (revised 13 September 2021)** (which mandates free-of-charge mutual data provision in Section 2(1) and explicitly recognizes Bundesbank's special user interest in real-economy and price statistics in Section 4) and the updated **ESS–ESCB MoU (signed 20 November 2024)** ([Bundesbank–Destatis MoU, 13 Sept. 2021](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/622452/607928e1af0a56a03c620202e65b5e65/mL/cooperation-agreement-between-the-bundesbank-data.pdf); [ESS–ESCB MoU, 20 Nov. 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/13019146/21116249/memorandum-understanding-ess_escb.pdf/4db8d61c-741c-8797-aa29-b563c5c2dcf2?t=1740567304257)).

Public dissemination and institutional transmission are sharply segregated. Publicly, Destatis exposes its **GENESIS-Online REST/JSON API** (`www-genesis.destatis.de/genesisWS/rest/2020/`) alongside Flat File CSV, XLSX, and official Python/VBA examples indexed by proprietary EVAS codes rather than native SDMX; critically, effective **15 July 2025**, Destatis retired all SOAP/XML web services and switched GENESIS-Online strictly to **POST-only** requests ([Destatis GENESIS-Online](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Service/OpenData/genesis-api-webservice-oberflaeche.html); [Destatis Official GitHub: GENESIS-Online](https://github.com/StatistischesBundesamt/GENESIS-Online)). Bundesbank, in turn, republishes German macro time series and real-time vintages publicly via an open, unauthenticated **SDMX Web Service REST API** (`api.statistiken.bundesbank.de/rest/`, serving SDMX-JSON, SDMX-ML 2.1, and CSV) alongside downloadable XLS/CSV bundles ([Bundesbank SDMX Web Service API](https://statistiken.bundesbank.de/statistiken-en/help-991452?article=bundesbank-sdmx-web-service-api-991448)).

Institutionally, primary European evidence documents three verified pillars of macro data exchange:

1. **Eurostat's eDAMIS** serves as the single entry point for NSI→Eurostat transmissions across EU Member States (including Destatis's ESA 2010 national accounts and HICP), with **more than 75%** of transmissions following **SDMX** as of November 2025, backed by pre-validation, encryption, and Euro SDMX Registry DSDs ([Eurostat CROS: Data Exchange in the ESS, Nov. 2025](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/data-exchange-ess); [Eurostat CROS: EDAMIS Short User Guide, Mar. 2026](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/edamis-short-user-guide)).
2. **Core macro sharing into the Eurosystem** is confirmed in primary ECB and Eurostat publications: **ECB Strategy Review Occasional Paper No. 265** confirms regular transmission of HICP data to the ESCB without naming the exact transmission route ([ECB Occasional Paper No. 265](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op265~a3fb0b611d.en.pdf)), while **Eurostat National Accounts** confirms national accounts (GDP) data sharing with the ECB ([Eurostat National Accounts Information & Data](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/national-accounts/information-data)).
3. **Confidential ESCB statistical exchanges** between NCBs (including Bundesbank) and the ECB run primarily over **ESCB-Net**—a closed, encrypted, sender-authenticated network carrying **SDMX-EDI (GESMES/TS)** and progressively **SDMX-ML** payloads with mandatory confidentiality flags and **zero confidentiality incidents recorded in 2024** ([ECB Statistical Confidentiality Report 2024](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/pdf/statisticalconfidentialityprotectionreport2024.en.pdf); [ECB SDMX Model](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_statistics/sdmx/html/index.en.html)).

Inside Frankfurt, Deutsche Bundesbank's Directorate General Statistics unites inbound institutional feeds (Destatis, Eurostat, ECB, BIS, OECD, IMF) and commercial feeds (**Markit, Thomson Reuters**, plus **Bloomberg, Datastream, and Haver Analytics** cited across publications) behind a Central Statistics Infrastructure and internal **SDMX Platform** spanning mainframe **`ZIS-DB`** (IMS HALDB/DB2), **`SDW`** (Oracle DB), **`DOTSTAT`** (MS SQL Server), and its **`IMIDIAS` / House of Microdata / RDSC** microdata ecosystem—serving internal analysts via BI tools and a database-linked **Excel connector** ([Stephan Müller / Bundesbank, BIS 9th IFC Conference, Aug. 2018](https://www.bis.org/events/20180830/ninth-biennial-ifc-conference-are-post-crisis-statistical-initiatives-completed/mueller-0.pdf); [BIS IFC Data-Sharing Practices](https://www.bis.org/publications/data-sharing-practices.pdf); [Bundesbank Financial Stability Review](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/654260/fe96f1e8092d9438ae5e190c23e20b8e/mL/2013-finanzstabilitaetsbericht-data.pdf)).

Bundesbank also co-developed **JDemetra+** with the National Bank of Belgium to execute X-13 seasonal and calendar adjustment on German HICP and macro series, with Destatis formally phasing out legacy X-12-ARIMA and BV4.1 by **October 2026** ([Bundesbank HICP Methodology](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/statistics/economic-activity-and-prices/harmonised-consumer-prices/methodology-and-quality-932144); [Destatis Seasonal Adjustment](https://www.destatis.de/EN/Methods/Seasonal-adjustment/_node.html)). Crucially, however, while eDAMIS and ESCB-Net are well documented at the EU level, the exact direct bilateral technical transport protocol between Destatis and Bundesbank for domestic GDP and CPI is not specified in the 2021 MoU (whose Section 1(4) supplementary agreements are non-public) or Müller (2018).

### South Korea compiles GDP inside the central bank while auditing CPI pre-releases

*Source notes: [south-korea-bok.md](notes/south-korea-bok.md)*

South Korea illustrates a rare **hybrid producer-consumer architecture** among advanced economies. Under **Article 86 of the Bank of Korea Act** and the Statistics Act, the **Bank of Korea (BoK)** directly compiles **National Accounts (GDP, Designated Statistic No. 301009)**, Input-Output tables, National Balance Sheets, **Producer Price Index (PPI)**, Balance of Payments, and Flow of Funds—splitting its Economic Statistics Department into **Economic Statistics Department 1** (financial, price, external, and enterprise statistics) and **Economic Statistics Department 2** (real-sector national accounts) in early 2025 to prepare for SNA 2025 ([BoK Statistical Release Schedule](https://www.bok.or.kr/portal/main/contents.do?menuNo=200998); [Edaily, 26 Dec. 2024](https://marketin.edaily.co.kr/News/ReadE?newsId=02952006639122456)). Meanwhile, Statistics Korea (KOSTAT)—elevated and renamed the **Ministry of Data and Statistics (MODS / 국가데이터처)** under the Prime Minister's Office on **1 October 2025**—compiles South Korea's **Consumer Price Index (CPI)**, Economically Active Population Survey, and Industrial Production ([MODS Launch Announcement, Oct. 2025](https://mods.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10301010000&bid=246&tag=&act=view&list_no=438851); [MODS CPI Portal](https://mods.go.kr/menu.es?mid=a20209010000)).

Because BoK compiles GDP as a derived statistic (`가공통계`), its upstream ingestion pipeline draws heavily on privileged inter-agency administrative microdata and foundational surveys governed by Statistics Act Articles 24/32 and formal BoK–KOSTAT MoUs ([BoK–KOSTAT MoU Archive](https://archives.bok.or.kr/archive/search/file/103636); [Korean National Law Information Center, Statistics Act](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=900542691&chrClsCd=010202)). Specifically, BoK ingests industrial/service output, household surveys, Economic Census, and CPI deflators from **MODS**; corporate, VAT, and personal income tax returns from the **National Tax Service (NTS)**—whose expanded Economic Census linkage lifted 2022 nominal GDP by **more than KRW 165 trillion** in the June 2024 benchmark revision; customs clearance records from the **Korea Customs Service (KCS)**; and direct firm/bank filings via BoK's dedicated **DACOS** (`통계자료입수시스템`) and electronic survey portals ([BoK 2020 Benchmark Revision, July 2024](https://www.bok.or.kr/portal/bbs/B0000217/view.do?nttId=10086131&menuNo=200144); [Yonhap Infomax, June 2024](https://news.einfomax.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=4312214); [BIS IFC WP No. 12](https://www.bis.org/publications/ifc-working-paper-12-balance-payments-statistics.pdf); [Digital Daily, Jan. 2020](https://m.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2020012208565629065)).

Internally, BoK replaced its legacy 2004 desktop SAS/Excel system with **New ECOS (`신ECOS`)** on **30 May 2022** (built with Bankware Global and Samsung SDS Brightics AI), layered an enterprise data catalog (**BIDAS**), and on **21 January 2026** launched **BOKI (`Bank Of Korea Intelligence`)**—built with Naver Cloud / HyperCLOVA X inside BoK's air-gapped on-premise network, including **`BIDAS.ai`** for natural-language data retrieval—while applying credit-card/ML nowcasting and relying on commercial vendors (Bloomberg, CEIC, Haver, LSEG/Refinitiv, Macrobond) for foreign macro data ([Hankyoreh, 30 May 2022](https://www.hani.co.kr/arti/economy/economy_general/1044968.html); [Yonhap Infomax, 21 Jan. 2026](https://news.einfomax.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=4394606); [Edaily, 21 Jan. 2026](https://marketin.edaily.co.kr/News/ReadE?newsId=03870406645320016); [BoK WP 2022-16](https://www.bok.or.kr/portal/bbs/P0002455/view.do?menuNo=500535&nttId=10072855)).

Publicly, MODS exposes finished CPI and cross-agency series via the **KOSIS Open API** (`kosis.kr/openapi`, returning JSON, **SDMX**, XLS, and XML at up to **200 requests/min** and **40,000 cells/req**) and hosts Korea's IMF SDDS Plus NSDP ([KOSIS Open API Guide](https://kosis.kr/serviceInfo/openAPIGuide.do); [MODS NSDP Portal](https://mods.go.kr/menu.es?mid=a20302010000)). BoK's **ECOS Open API** (`ecos.bok.or.kr/api/`) serves custom REST JSON/XML (rather than native SDMX) and re-disseminates MODS CPI alongside BoK statistics—explicitly flagging potential **publication lags** relative to MODS and dual copyright ([ECOS Open API](https://ecos.bok.or.kr/api/); [ECOS Portal](https://ecos.bok.or.kr/)).

Most strikingly, South Korea exposes a **verified discrepancy between international metadata and domestic statutory pre-release**: Korea's IMF DSBB CPI metadata asserts that *"no one outside Statistics Korea has access to the data before their release to the public"* ([IMF DSBB Korea CPI Metadata](https://dsbb.imf.org/sdds/dqaf-base/country/KOR/category/CPI00)), yet **Statistics Act Article 27-2** permits logged statutory pre-release (`사전제공`) for official policy duties, and an **October 2023 National Assembly audit** (covering May 2022–August 2023) documented **620 KOSTAT pre-releases across 33 institutions**, including **54 pre-releases to the Bank of Korea** and **71 pre-releases of CPI** across MOEF, the Presidential Office, and BoK, mirrored by BoK's own annual pre-release logs under Article 27-2(5) ([Statistics Act Art. 27-2](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=900542691&chrClsCd=010202); [JoongAng Ilbo, 12 Oct. 2023](https://www.joongang.co.kr/article/25198803); [BoK Pre-Released Statistics Notice](https://www.bok.or.kr/portal/bbs/P0000561/view.do?nttId=10076503&menuNo=200037)). Despite extensive public documentation of DACOS, New ECOS, and BIDAS, the exact low-level bilateral transfer or ETL protocol moving raw NTS/KCS microdata and MODS CPI into BoK's ECOS and BIDAS environments is not publicly disclosed.

## Conclusion

Across the four primary OECD cases examined—Australia, the United States, Germany/Eurosystem, and South Korea—and the United Kingdom pre-release benchmark, primary evidence shows that **public NSO APIs are far better documented for external dissemination than for internal central bank ingestion**. Within these jurisdictions, documented data flows are shaped first by **legal and institutional boundaries**—such as ABS lock-up rules, OMB Directive No. 3, ONS exceptional pre-release letters, South Korea's statutory compilation of GDP inside the Bank of Korea, and Europe's ESS/ESCB framework—and only second by REST or SDMX availability. Public APIs (ABS Indicator API, BEA/BLS REST, Destatis POST-only GENESIS, KOSIS SDMX) are clearly documented for external users, while domestic institutional pipes (outside eDAMIS and ESCB-Net at the EU layer) remain largely undocumented in public primary sources.

Across these jurisdictions and the 19-institution BIS IFC time-series survey, the clearest documented modernization trend through 2026–2027 is **decoupling governed data storage from analytical consumption inside the central bank firewall**. Initiatives such as the Federal Reserve Board's DDP-to-FRED migration, the Bank of England's Future FAME programme, the RBA's CoreMod/EDP overhaul, and the Bank of Korea's New ECOS, BIDAS, and air-gapped BOKI/`BIDAS.ai` deployments point away from monolithic FAME, SAS, and standalone desktop-Excel workflows toward unified repositories serving Python, R, BI, and database-linked Excel connectors. For practitioners auditing or designing macro ingestion pipelines in these jurisdictions, verifying statutory pre-release logs and revision-vintage handling matters as much as tracking public NSO APIs—and public NSO APIs should never be assumed to be the central bank's own internal transport without primary evidence.

### Design rationale for the ABSdataAPI Phase 1 pipeline

The cross-country evidence and the live API checks in [ABS API research notes](../abs-api-research.md) directly motivate the three core mechanisms of the [§9 Phase 1 PoC architecture](../abs-api-research.md#9-suggested-poc-architecture):

1. **Bounded date-window pulls (`startPeriod`/`endPeriod`) on the keyless Data API:** Embargo and equality-of-access regimes decouple public NSO APIs from privileged internal feeds, while public APIs enforce strict operational limits and breaking changes (BEA and BLS quotas, Destatis's POST-only cutover, KOSIS cell caps). At the ABS, the production Indicator API requires a manual key request (~1-week turnaround) and covers only 22 whole-dataset flows with no custom dimensional filters, whereas the keyless SDMX 2.1 Data API (Beta) carries undocumented post-embargo arrival timing (`~12:30 pm` is unverified), documented **30-second timeout and 10 MB response-size gateway limits**, and an unreliable **`updatedAfter`** parameter ([ABS API research notes](../abs-api-research.md)). Phase 1 therefore polls after 12:00 pm Canberra time using bounded `startPeriod`/`endPeriod` slices rather than `updatedAfter`, deferring Indicator API evaluation to Phase 2 once a key is obtained.
2. **Provenance metadata, codelist resolution, and complete-response validation:** Central banks across all studied jurisdictions maintain governed metadata and mapping layers over raw NSO feeds—from the RBA's series mapping and source attribution in Tables G1, G4, and H1 to the Fed Board's 15 FAME metadata attributes, Bundesbank's SDMX Platform, and FRED Data Desk file-integrity checks—while decoupling central storage from Python, R, and BI analytics. Because ABS Data API dimension codes are numeric, dataflow versions differ (e.g., active `CPI` 2.0.0 and reinstated `CPI_Q` 1.0.0 alongside ceased `CPI_M` 1.2.0), and 30 s / 10 MB gateway limits risk mid-transfer truncation, Phase 1 resolves codelists to human-readable labels, checks expected vs. actual row counts per slice, and writes CSV/Parquet alongside `fetch_timestamp`, `dataflow_version`, and `response_size` provenance metadata.
3. **Retained revision vintages via `(key, OBS_VALUE)` snapshots:** Public NSO APIs typically overwrite revised historical observations rather than exposing point-in-time history, which is why central banks maintain dedicated vintage databases (such as St. Louis Fed **ALFRED**, Bundesbank's real-time database, and the Fed Board's snapshot stores), license commercial PIT aggregators, and distinguish substantive revisions from re-referencing (as the RBA documents in *Changes to Statistical Tables*). Phase 1 therefore persists every `(key, OBS_VALUE)` observation and diffs across polls to record new series values, revisions, and gaps without relying on server-side `updatedAfter` state.
